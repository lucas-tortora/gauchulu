# CHAPTER 12 — EL LIBRO

### _The Book_

**Goal**: Run the transport (or the siege), the reveal, and the final choice. End the campaign on a player decision with named cost — Hidden, Held, or the silence that becomes Opened — and live with what remains. **Emotional arc**: Cold resolve → The long hour → Choice → Aftermath. **Key seed**: He told you the truth, mostly. The trap is not in what he said — it is in what he didn't. The book only has to cross the threshold of the vault's diagram; the choice is whether you give him that — or whether you simply run out of time to refuse.

> **Keeper note.** Four acts (~90 minutes each): **the transport (or siege)**, **the reveal**, **the choice**, **the aftermath**. Run them in order; don't collapse them. **El Patrón's reasonableness is the point** — he is courteous in the parlor, on Calle Belgrano, at the chest; the horror is that you almost agree with him, so never make him hiss or threaten. **The Choice belongs to the players** — do not steer; the campaign has been asking this question for twelve chapters. **At this table it's Hide, Held, or Opened** — Use never opened (no Reader), and Destroyed isn't run this table (Mercedes has no ritual for it — she'd have used it already if she did). Opened isn't a third thing to pitch the players; it's what's already happening if they don't choose one of the other two in time. And **the Marks pay differently in each ending** — make the players feel the Mark's role at the end, including the one ending where the Mark's role is simply to go out.

> **Handout**: [`city-grid.svg`](assets/city-grid.svg) — the 1580 eleven-by-nine grid with the Manzana de las Luces (vault), Plaza de Mayo, Cabildo, Riachuelo, dock, and the brig, the route highlighted, the three ambush corners marked (all three uncovered — there's no cordon this table), the dual diagram faintly overlaid and centered on the vault. As the carriage moves and the byakhee's chase lights up street-lamps ahead of it, **mark each completed leg on the players' copy. They are _drawing the diagram_ — let them see it being drawn.**

## ⚠ Six things to settle BEFORE the players arrive

1. **Use is closed, Destroyed isn't run.** No Reader at this table (the C5 page-79 seed was never planted) — Use never opens. And Mercedes has no destroy-ritual to hand over: **if she knew how to end the book outright, she'd have done it decades ago.** **The Choice at this table is Hide, [Held](#option-2--held), or [Opened](#option-3--opened).**
2. **Two Marked PCs now — the Antiquarian and Negro Camba.** [Held](#option-2--held) needs at least one of them to volunteer to cross to the other side of the diagram and spend the rest of their life holding it shut from there. Either can go, both can go, or the option can be declined entirely (Hide is always there instead). Decide nothing in advance — this is a live table choice, same as the old Destroyed tiers used to be, just pulpier and without a forced death.
3. **[Opened](#option-3--opened) is real, not a scare tactic.** It's what happens by default if the sixty seconds run out with nobody committing to Hide or Held, or if a player just hands the book over. Don't soften it and don't railroad the table away from it — a table that freezes, argues past the clock, or genuinely decides to let him win should get that ending, played straight.
4. **Pull the session-1 decision log** ([the table in C11](chapter-11.md#-decision-log--write-these-down-c12-reads-them-back)): docks contributions, tunnel reported?, Ana's intel, Gaucho's terms, Warding, cordon count, Quirce, falsified tally. **At this table every one of these is empty/no** — the log is blank across the board, which is itself the information: nothing was prepared, and now there's no time left to fix it before dawn.
5. **Which branch to run** depends on [the vote](#the-vote): **[the transport, degraded](#act-1--the-transport)** if they move it, **[the siege](#alternate-act-1--the-siege-at-the-vault)** if they don't. Both trigger at **dawn**, both land at the same Act 2 (the reveal) and Act 3 (the Choice) — decide before the table sits down which one you're prepping harder for, but keep the other's beat list in reach.
6. **Two new NPCs, one scene each, before any of the above**: **Anacleto Bazán**, the locksmith Galíndez brought in to crack the second-patio chest — dead in the blast — and **Cosme**, his nephew and apprentice, who comes looking for him that evening. Neither is in `REF-NPCs.md`; both are written out in full below, where they appear.

## Standing rules — know these cold, don't look them up

1. **⛔ No rolls in El Patrón's presence — ever.** The [silence tell](#midnight--the-parlor-same-night) replaces the dice: the Marks go quiet before he arrives. At WP2 that silence is what tells the Marked PCs he's rounding the corner before he's seen.
2. **He never hisses, looms, or threatens.** Courteous in the parlor, courteous on Calle Belgrano, courteous at the chest. **The horror is that you almost agree with him.** If he ever sounds like a monster, you've lost the scene.
3. **The Choice belongs to the players.** Do not argue for an option, do not have an NPC argue past their one line, do not impose a clock beyond Mercedes' 60 seconds. If they stall, silence is the correct Keeper move.
4. **Held needs a volunteer, not a ritual.** No parchment, no prop, no Mercedes-taught formula — it's the Marked PCs' own standing connection to the geometry that lets them cross and hold. **If a Marked player reaches for it, it's theirs to take.**
5. **The transport (or the siege) is the trap, and they know it going in.** Whichever branch runs, they've been told exactly what tonight costs. Don't play the reveal as a gotcha — play it as the thing they already accepted.
6. **The byakhee harries, it doesn't resolve anything.** It's pressure and spectacle for WP2 and the road to WP3 — El Patrón is the actual threat, and he never needs to hurry.

## ⭐ THE CHOICE — one page, don't fumble this

**Say this, in the players' hearing, then stop talking:**

> Mercedes, to the table: _"Este es el momento. Escóndanlo, o denle a los Marcados lo que están pidiendo. Decidan."_ **Sixty seconds. If nobody's decided anything by the end of them, that's a decision too — go to Opened.**

|  | **HIDE** | **HELD** | **OPENED** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **What they do** | The chest goes **forward** — onto the launch, to the brig, across the river to a new vault in **Montevideo** | One or both Marked PCs step through the crack in the geometry, to the other side of the diagram | Nobody hides it, nobody crosses — the chest opens inside the geometry anyway |
| **Who acts** | **Albarrán** speaks the Latin (Mercedes may take it instead, if the players ask) | **The Antiquarian and/or Negro Camba** — a real, personal choice for each of them | **El Patrón**, once it's clear no one else is going to act |
| **The cost** | **Whoever speaks the Latin dies within 24 hours.** Mercedes also loses an eye over the next month | **Not death.** They live out their full remaining life on the other side, holding the door shut — gone from this one, for good | **Everyone, evenly.** SAN 1/1D10; the Marks go out for good |
| **El Patrón** | **Not destroyed.** Withdraws north to Areco and his other names. Back, but not in their lifetimes | **Locked out for as long as they hold.** Not gone, not banished — physically denied the door while the volunteer(s) live | **Completes.** The rest of the avatar comes through; he thanks them and walks away |
| **The book** | Survives, crosses to Montevideo, a new vault, fresh consecration | Stays in its chest here — the crossing is what matters, not the book itself | Gone with him. No Keeper will ever see it again |
| **The name it costs** | _"Hic locus est. Hic manet."_ — the third and final saying of _Por la tierra que fue buena_ | No prayer — the Marked don't need one. Just the step, and the crack closing behind them | No prayer either — nobody gets a last word here except him |

**Hide and Held are both available; nothing needs to be earned first, and they aren't mutually exclusive** — a table that wants both a relocated book and a held door can have it (Hide's cost still lands, Held's volunteer(s) still cross), with no special bonus for doing so. **Opened isn't a third choice to weigh alongside them — it's what's already happening the moment the other two aren't.** Don't present it as a button to press; let it be the silence that wins by default.

## COLD OPEN — THE EXPLOSION

> **This replaces the written cold open below**, which assumed a peaceful day-before that never happened at this table. Open session 2 *here*, in the smoke, with no recap — same rule as C11's own cold open: the players lived the vault that morning; they don't need it summarized back to them.

> _El sonido no es un estampido, es más un golpe — algo que se siente en el pecho antes de escucharse. Para cuando el humo se despeja lo suficiente para ver, **Anacleto Bazán** está contra la pared del fondo, quieto de una manera que ya no admite duda. El cofre — abierto al fin, la tapa arrancada de una de sus bisagras — humea despacio, los tres candados esparcidos por el piso, dos todavía cerrados. Todo lo demás en el segundo patio está roto, volcado, o ardiendo despacio en un rincón. A todos les pitan los oídos igual._

**Anacleto Bazán, cerrajero** — dead in the blast, the one non-Custodio in the room. Galíndez brought him in: a man he's used before, quietly, for locks that needed opening without a warrant behind them. Fifty-something, missing two fingers on his left hand already — an old story about a strongbox in Montevideo he liked to tell, and won't tell again. He worked with a small charge of powder packed into the weakest lock's mechanism, the way he'd done a dozen times before without incident, and this time it took the whole chest, and him with it. **A mundane death, same as Tránsito's** — no entity, no geometry, just bad luck and too much powder in a room too small. Give it that weight rather than a mythos one.

**Galíndez was standing closest** — he vouched for Bazán, stayed to watch the work himself rather than send a soldier. That's why he's hurt worst. If a player asks him how he's holding up, one line, no more: _"Yo lo traje. Eso es todo lo que necesito pensar por ahora."_

**First aid, before anyone else shows up.** Nobody sits with the shock — whoever has Medicine or First Aid gets to work immediately, on Galíndez first, then each other: shirts torn into strips, a flask of something stronger than water for the worst of it, hands not quite steady doing careful work anyway. **Roll it out**: one Medicine/First Aid check per patient is plenty — this isn't a scene to stage line by line, it's texture for the half hour that follows. Mercedes, hurt herself, directs more than she helps: _"Eso primero. Lo demás espera."_ **This is the half hour [the Custodios' knock](#the-custodios-arrive) below interrupts** — the room still smells of blood and spent powder, bandages still being tied off, when the first knock lands.

## THE CUSTODIOS ARRIVE

**The blast draws exactly the attention Mercedes has spent a career avoiding.** Within the half hour, knocking — then more knocking — at the street door: three or four of the order's other members, the ones who live close enough in the barrio to have heard it, alarmed and half-dressed, wanting to know what happened to the house where their senior Keeper died less than a day ago.

> _Se abren paso al segundo patio sin esperar respuesta — gente mayor en su mayoría, batas y sobretodos puestos apurados sobre ropa de dormir, una vela cada uno porque nadie pensó en un candil. Se quedan en la puerta del patio, mirando el cofre roto, el cuerpo de un hombre que ninguno de ellos reconoce, y a Mercedes, sentada en el piso con la espalda contra la pared, sin apurarse a levantarse._

**This is the political cost of the night, not a mechanical one** — run it as pressure, not a fight. A useful voice: **Don Casimiro Vega**, a Custodio in his seventies, cautious by temperament, one of the fourteen chairs at that long pine table for longer than Mercedes has. He says, in front of everyone, the thing Saráchaga can't anymore:

> _"Un extraño. En esta casa. La misma noche que enterramos a Eladio. Y ahora está muerto también, y el cofre de la orden abierto en el piso como una fruta podrida."_ Se vuelve hacia Mercedes, no hostil, algo peor — decepcionado. _"Eladio decía que moverlo era el error. Yo empiezo a pensar que se quedó corto. Quizás el error fue siempre tocarlo. Cualquier cosa."_

**He doesn't win the argument tonight — nobody does.** But he's the first person in the whole campaign to make the *"leave it alone entirely"* case out loud, to Mercedes' face, in front of the players. **Let his voice stand as the other half of [the vote](#the-vote) later tonight** — Mercedes has been arguing alone up to now; she shouldn't be anymore. If a player wants to press him, he has one more line, quieter: _"No los culpo a ustedes. Culpo a cuarenta años de gente que decidió que esto era su problema en vez de dejarlo donde estaba."_

**Mechanically**: no rolls, no combat — this is a roomful of frightened, grieving old men and women, not a threat. Give the players (and Mercedes, and a hurt Galíndez) the work of managing it: explaining Bazán, explaining Saráchaga, holding the room together. It costs time and goodwill, not blood. By the time it's over, it's full dark.

## THAT EVENING — THE ASSISTANT

> _Ya de noche, un golpe distinto en la puerta — más débil, más inseguro que los anteriores. Es un chico, dieciséis años, la ropa de trabajo todavía puesta, una bolsa de herramientas de cuero colgada del hombro que le queda grande. **Cosme**, sobrino y aprendiz de Bazán. Su tío salió a un trabajo esa mañana y no volvió a la casa a comer, ni a cerrar el taller, ni a nada. Preguntó en dos direcciones antes de que alguien le dijera, sin querer, hacia dónde había ido._

**Cosme** doesn't know what the Custodios are, what the chest held, or what his uncle actually did for a living beyond "locks." He knows Bazán mentioned "un trabajo para el capitán" before he left, and he knows his uncle hasn't come home. He is not going to leave without an answer, and he is not going to be talked down the way Luisa was — he isn't scared of the players, he's scared *for* someone, which is a different thing to manage.

> - Si le dicen que no saben nada: no se lo cree, ni por un segundo. *"Mi tío no desaparece. Nunca. Díganme dónde está."*
> - Si alguien intenta despedirlo con plata sin explicación: la mira, no la toca. *"No vine a cobrar nada. Vine a buscarlo a él."*
> - Si le dicen la verdad: se queda muy quieto un momento largo, y después una sola pregunta, muy baja: *"¿Lo dejaron solo?"*

**A real decision, no correct answer** — same pattern as Luisa, as Ana, as everyone else this campaign has asked the players to be honest or not with:

- **Tell him the truth.** He's owed a body to bury and a story that makes sense of it, even the strange parts. Costs: a civilian outside the order now knows a Keeper meeting house exists, that his uncle died opening a locked chest inside it, and that "El Patrón," Saráchaga, and a dead senior Keeper are all connected. Mercedes may object, quietly, once he's gone. **Gain**: Cosme is sixteen, grieving, sharp-handed, and knows his uncle's trade — a live thread for later if you ever want a locksmith's apprentice again, in this campaign or the next one.
- **Send him away with a story.** Bazán took ill on the job, or never arrived, or left for the campo like Lacasa — pick one and Galíndez or Mercedes delivers it smoothly, practiced liars both. Costs nothing tonight. **Risk**: a sixteen-year-old who doesn't believe the story keeps asking, at exactly the worst week for the order to be asked questions.
- **Recruit him quietly** — Galíndez's instinct, if a player doesn't take the lead: offer Cosme paid work finishing his uncle's outstanding jobs, under the order's eye, in exchange for silence and access. Costs a promise the Custodios now owe a child; buys a controlled version of the truth instead of an uncontrolled one.

> _Imagen final de la escena: Cosme se va — solo, o acompañado, según lo que decidieron — cargando la bolsa de herramientas de su tío como si pesara mucho más que antes. Nadie en el patio dice nada hasta que sus pasos se pierden en la calle._

## THE VOTE

**Run this the same night** — right after Cosme leaves, everyone patched up, nobody fully healed, Galíndez worst of the lot, and now with **Don Casimiro's words from earlier tonight** sitting in the room alongside Mercedes'. **This replaces the scripted resolution.** In the written chapter, Albarrán walks in wet and says *"Dios me contestó. Lo movemos"* and the vote is over before it's taken. That line depends on the Ana beat, which didn't run — so there is no NPC answer waiting for them this time. **This is a real table vote, with no correct answer, run the same way Act 3's Choice is run later in this chapter: put it on the table in the players' words, then stop talking.**

Mercedes gathers them at the meeting-house table — the same one, the chest (Saráchaga's, emptied, the vault's untouched) still visible through the doorway to the second patio.

> _Mercedes no se sienta. Tiene la voz más cansada que enojada, aunque lo segundo también está ahí, abajo. Nadie mencionó a Casimiro todavía, pero todos piensan en lo que dijo._

She lays out exactly where they are, reusing the argument she's been making since the vault, because now it's not hypothetical:

> _"¿Dejarlo? Eladio dijo eso durante cuarenta años, y tuvo razón cuarenta años. Pero anoche estuvo_ **caliente**_, y no la vio caliente en toda su vida — la vio una sola vez, unas horas antes de morirse, y lo primero que escribió fue 'movámoslo'. Eso no es una piedra cansada. Eso es una mano apretando."_
>
> _"Y fíjense en lo peor: si nos quedamos quietos no estamos ganando tiempo — le estamos regalando la hora a él. Va a romper la bóveda una noche que elija él, no nosotros, y nos va a encontrar durmiendo, sin cordón, sin gente, sin nada en la mano."_

**Let that last line land without comment — it isn't rhetorical anymore.** No cordon, no Cursed Gaucho, an injured Galíndez, a dead outsider, a room full of frightened Custodios: that's not a hypothetical worst case, it's this week, whichever way they vote.

**If a player raises Casimiro's argument, Mercedes doesn't dismiss it**: _"Casimiro tiene razón en una cosa. Tocarlo fue el error, hace cuarenta años, cuando yo lo copié. Ese error ya está hecho. Lo que decidimos ahora no es si tocarlo — es qué hacemos con haberlo tocado."_

**She also says, flatly, what they don't have**: _"Ana. Quirce. No sé cuánto sabe él ya. No tuvimos tiempo de averiguarlo."_ — the leak was never caught, and neither Mercedes nor the players know if El Patrón is already ahead of whatever they decide. Don't resolve this either way; let it sit as a live unknown through the finale.

**Then she gives the one required piece this table's cut cloister scene ([C11](chapter-11.md#doña-mercedes-alone)) would otherwise have delivered** — the cost of Hide, load-bearing for [Act 3](#-the-choice--one-page-dont-fumble-this):

> Unprompted: _"Si lo escondemos de nuevo, Ramón sabe la fórmula latina. Se la aprendió a Saráchaga en 1798. No es magia. Es un intercambio: quien la diga, no ve pasar el día siguiente."_ **The Hide ending now has a known price before anyone chooses it — and not only the obvious one.** Mercedes keeps talking, because the death isn't the part she needs them to understand:
>
> _"Y hay algo más. Esconderlo no es terminarlo — es un préstamo. En Montevideo va a haber Custodios que todavía no nacen, que no saben nada de esto, que van a heredar el libro sin haber elegido cargarlo, igual que a mí me lo heredaron a los veinticuatro años sin que nadie me preguntara. Nosotros no cerramos nada esta noche. Se lo pasamos a otro."_

**The downside of Hide isn't only a death — it's that Hide doesn't fix anything.** Say this plainly if a player asks what's actually wrong with hiding it: the book survives, El Patrón survives, and two hundred and eleven years of Custodio history simply restarts somewhere else, with new people paying the same slow price starting from zero. And Casimiro's faction doesn't forget this decision either: some of the fourteen chairs stop showing up to the meeting house after tonight, and Mercedes spends what's left of her life managing an order that no longer fully trusts her judgment — a cost paid in relationships, not blood.

**She has nothing to offer for the other direction, and says so if asked.** No parchment, no destroy-ritual — Mercedes never carried one. If a player asks whether there's another way, flat: _"Si yo supiera cómo terminar con el libro del todo, créanme, lo habría hecho hace treinta años. Lo único que sé hacer es esconderlo mejor."_ **What actually answers "another way" is the Marked PCs' own option** — see [Held](#option-2--held) — which isn't hers to grant; it's theirs, if they want it, and she may not even know it's possible until she watches it happen.

*(If a player asks how she came to carry the forty years of guilt behind all this, the full confession is still written out in [C11's cloister section](chapter-11.md#doña-mercedes-alone) — read from it directly rather than re-staging the scene.)*

## MIDNIGHT — THE PARLOR, SAME NIGHT

> _La sala de Inocencia de noche es chica y tibia: una mesa de madera oscura, dos sillas de asiento de cuero, un aparador con loza desportillada, y en el medio de la mesa una hogaza de pan que nadie cortó todavía. Un candil bajo, casi consumido, deja más sombra que luz en las esquinas. Todo en el cuarto es ordinario — y por eso mismo, cuando algo no lo es, se nota de inmediato._

The players return to Inocencia's once the vote is taken. Bread on the table. And a **visitor** in Inocencia's chair — a man they have never seen and have met in a hundred small forms across four arcs. Dressed simply, not large, his face unremarkable in a way that should be impossible (the eye keeps trying to find a feature to remember, and fails). He stands, bows slightly:

> _"Qué bueno conocernos al fin. Soy Don Eligio Dadálah. Ustedes me conocen como El Patrón."_

Alone, unarmed, here to **talk**. **He already knows which way they voted** — his opening line branches on it:

- **If they voted to move it**: _"Me dijeron que van a moverlo al amanecer. Bien. Es exactamente lo que necesito — que se mueva. Háganlo, y al final de la mañana los dejo vivir."_
- **If they voted to keep it below**: _"Entonces lo van a dejar abajo. Está bien. Ya se lo dije a Mercedes: voy a tener que romper la bóveda yo mismo. Al amanecer, entonces — abajo, en vez de en la calle. Eso también va a costarles más que un viaje de carruaje."_

> **Why dawn, not right now?** A fair question a table will actually ask — have the answer ready, two-layered. **The mundane layer, already on record**: Borghi's own condition from the day-before material — _"A la primera luz, zarpo. Con carga o sin carga."_ — the brig isn't moving before first light no matter when the chest reaches the dock, so there's no tactical gain in a midnight departure, only hours spent sitting exposed instead of moving once the ship can actually leave. **The layer underneath it, his**: he names the hour himself, in both branches, before anyone can suggest otherwise — because the hour isn't really theirs to pick. Dawn is when he wants this, and everything about tonight's terms was already on his schedule, not theirs. If a player pushes to move at midnight anyway, against what he just said, that's a real and legitimate table choice — see the note at [Dawn — departure](#dawn--departure).

He is telling the truth, mostly — he _will_ let them live if the move happens, and _will_ break the vault if they refuse. The choice was always real; tonight it's just been named twice, once by the players and once by him. **The trap he doesn't name**: he needs the book _outside the vault's diagram_, not stationary inside it — that is what lets him take it. He will not threaten Mercedes here, name Lacasa, or promise specifics. At the door, to the player he addressed most:

> _"Doña Mercedes ha cargado esto sola durante cuarenta años. La quiero, ¿saben? Es de las pocas personas que me han hecho trabajar. Cuando esto termine, mañana, voy a pedirles un favor: déjenmela viva. Yo la voy a tratar bien."_

He bows again. The door closes on its own. **SAN Check**: 1/1D6 — a single conversation with the avatar of Nyarlathotep, in a boarding-house parlor, with bread on the table.

**Descripción física**: estatura mediana, contextura mediana, ropa simple y prolija — nada que llamaría la atención en cualquier salón porteño. La cara es el problema: correcta, agradable, y absolutamente imposible de retener. El ojo busca un rasgo para recordar — la forma de la nariz, el color exacto de los ojos — y no lo encuentra, una y otra vez, en el mismo rostro. Sonríe con facilidad, como quien disfruta genuinamente de la conversación. No hay nada amenazante en su postura. Eso es lo que asusta.

**Diálogo — si los jugadores insisten en preguntar**:
> - *"¿Quién es usted, en verdad?"* — sonríe, casi con cariño. *"Soy exactamente quien les dije que era: Eligio Dadálah. La pregunta que en realidad quieren hacer es otra, y ya saben la respuesta."*
> - *"¿Por qué no nos amenaza?"* — *"Porque no hace falta. Y porque las amenazas son para quien no tiene tiempo. Yo tengo todo el tiempo que quiera — ustedes, esta noche, no."*
> - Si un PJ se muestra tentado por la oferta (dejar el libro donde está, o entregarlo directamente): no presiona, no insiste — se limita a sonreír un poco más. *"No hace falta que decidan nada esta noche que ya no hayan decidido. Yo puedo esperar hasta el amanecer. Ya esperé cuarenta años."*
> - Si le preguntan por Mercedes: la calidez en su voz, por un instante, parece genuina. *"Es de las pocas personas que me han hecho trabajar de verdad. Eso no es un cumplido menor, viniendo de mí."*
> - Despedida, poniéndose de pie: *"Nos vemos al amanecer, entonces. Con el libro afuera del dibujo, de una forma u otra."*

> ⚠️ **El Patrón = no roll — the silence tell.** Within ~20 m of him the Marks go **silent**: the city-hum the Marked have carried since [C10's grid entry](chapter-10.md#entry-into-the-city) stops dead. The absence _is_ the detection — they feel him as a hole in the geometry, the one shape the grid cannot read; it resumes when the door closes. **This pays off at WP2** — the Marks go silent before he rounds the corner, and again before the byakhee arrives.

**After he's gone**: Inocencia is asleep at the kitchen table, head on her arms — she has been asleep since before he arrived; no one heard him come in. She wakes with a small gasp at the closing door and does not remember him. On the chair where he sat: a single thin object — **a pencil**, new, recently sharpened. Not threatening. A gift, or a joke, or a calling card. The players carry it; nothing they do with it produces any effect. It is just a pencil. _(Don't explain it. El Patrón's small kindnesses are more frightening than his violence.)_

> _Mercedes, que llega poco después, escucha a los jugadores describir la visita sin interrumpir, y después: "Bueno. Al amanecer hacemos lo que decidimos. Y cuando llegue el momento, decidimos si dejarlo vivo a él, no a mí." Un reloj en algún lugar de la calle Belgrano da la hora — y la campanada suena, y suena, y no para hasta diez segundos más de lo que una sola campanada debería durar. Después, silencio. En algún cuarto de la casa, alguien encuentra, sobre una mesa de luz, un objeto que nadie recuerda haber dejado ahí: el pequeño disco de bronce de una piedra-testigo. Está caliente._

**A short, quiet wait follows** — an hour or two of trying to rest that doesn't really work, no one sleeping much, the hot witness-stone sitting wherever it was left. Then: dawn. **Go straight to whichever branch [the vote](#the-vote) chose.**

### Weak Point 2 — Belgrano and Defensa — the byakhee

> _La esquina no tiene nada de especial a simple vista — una casa de altos, una pulpería cerrada con tranca, el empedrado más parejo que en el resto de la cuadra porque acá pasa más carro que en ningún otro cruce del barrio. **No one is waiting for them here.** The Cursed Gaucho was never told the plan — never recruited, never even approached — so there is no one to meet El Patrón at this corner but the party itself. The carriage slows to make the turn — the corner Galíndez feared most — and he is simply there, around it, walking unhurried up the middle of the street. Alone, simply dressed, smiling:

> _"Buen día. Solos, entonces. Está bien — no hacía falta nadie más para esto."_

**He doesn't fight.** He raises one hand, almost lazily, and says something short, not in Spanish, not in any language a player would recognize — and the dawn sky over the rooftops tears.

> _Arriba, contra la primera luz gris, algo se despliega donde no había nada un segundo antes — alas de cuero mucho más grandes que las de cualquier pájaro, un cuerpo a medio camino entre insecto y algo peor, y un chillido que no debería poder producir ningún animal, un sonido que se siente en los dientes antes que en los oídos. **SAN Check**: 1/1D6 — the first sight of a byakhee, dropping out of a Buenos Aires dawn.

**El Patrón sends it after them and keeps walking.** He doesn't need to hurry — he never has. _"Los va a acompañar el resto del camino. Yo los alcanzo en el muelle."_ He falls behind at an ordinary walking pace, entirely unconcerned, while the thing above the carriage decides who to go for first.

**Run this as a chase, not a stand** — 3 rounds, escalating, covering the rest of the route to the dock (fold the remaining block-cadence Hound rolls into it rather than running both separately):
- **Round 1**: it circles, testing — a dive at the lead horses or the carriage roof. **DEX/Dodge** for whoever's exposed; a miss costs a soldier a bad claw-wound, not a death.
- **Round 2**: it goes for the chest itself, or for whichever player looks most like a threat — **Fighting/Firearms** if someone shoots back; it's fast and doesn't need to press an attack home to do its job, which is to terrify and slow them, not to kill.
- **Round 3**: as the dock comes into view it peels off, screaming once, and is gone — its work done. **If nobody drove it off**, it simply loses interest once El Patrón is close enough to the water; **if the players hurt it badly**, it flees the same way, just earlier.

**Keeper — what it's for.** This isn't a fight to be won so much as endured. It should cost blood, time, and nerve, not the transport's success — the transport was already lost the moment the vote decided to move without a cordon. Let a soldier get hurt or nearly thrown from the carriage roof; let a player's shot go wide; let the sky feel wrong for three straight minutes. **Stats**: [full block in `assets/npc-stats.md`](assets/npc-stats.md#byakhee) — Fighting 55%, damage 1D6+DB, HP 14. **Claws only, never Bite and Hold** — the drain maneuver can gut a PC's STR in two rounds and isn't what this encounter is for; let it strike and back off, not latch on.

### Weak Point 3 — the dock

The carriage rolls onto the Riachuelo dock with the byakhee's scream still fading behind them, and Galíndez says nothing for a moment, just listens to it go.

El bergantín *Carmen del Pilar* espera a cincuenta metros, dos faroles de popa marcando su posición en el agua todavía oscura; más allá, apenas visible, la goleta señuelo *La Niña Encantada* ya larga amarras rumbo a ningún lado en particular — más allá de ambas, río de por medio, está Montevideo, adonde el cajón debería estar yendo. The launch waits, Capitán Borghi at the tiller, hand on his pistol since he heard something scream over the rooftops. **The chest is offloaded.** And **this is where El Patrón takes the book** — he arrives at the dock on foot, unhurried, entirely unmarked, the byakhee wheeling once overhead before it drops onto the ridge of a warehouse roof to watch, folded and still:

> _"Gracias. Esto era lo único que hacía falta — que cruzara el umbral de la bóveda. El camino no importa; ese lo eligió Galíndez, no ustedes. Tenía la llave hace cuarenta años. Lo único que me faltaba era esto: el libro afuera del dibujo."_

**Diálogo — si los jugadores le hablan directamente, antes del Acto 2**:

And **the geometry activates** — this is what the byakhee's chase was really doing, the whole way here: at each block it crossed, the street-lamps flickered in sequence three ahead, then two, then one, light arriving before the carriage did (Galíndez, low, to no one in particular: _"Las farolas. # ACT 3 — THE CHOICE

The campaign's final scene. Three options — two the players choose, one that just happens if they choose neither in time. **The decision comes from the players, not any single PC** — take a table vote if needed. Hide and Held aren't exclusive — see the note at the end of [Held](#option-2--held) — but there's no bonus ending for taking both, and no way to combine either with [Opened](#option-3--opened), which is what's running the instant nothing else is.

> ⊕ **The Antiquarian at the Choice.** Held is, for them, the strangest possible gift: a seeker who crossed an ocean to _understand_ the beyond-science is being offered a permanent seat on the other side of the door, closer to it than any scholarship could get them, at the cost of everything they came from. Expect them to be genuinely torn — this is either the worst trade of their life or the only one that was ever going to satisfy them. **Hidden** should read, to this PC specifically, as the smaller and sadder option: understanding deferred, not attempted.

## Option 1 — Hidden

> Complete the crossing anyway — the chest goes onto the launch, to the brig, across the river to a new vault in **Montevideo**. Not Buenos Aires this time: a different city, a different Keeper network, a fresh grid El Patrón has spent no forty years learning. The book survives; El Patrón is denied — but _not_ gone.

**Mercedes leads** — she waves Galíndez's men to keep loading, not to stop; the chest goes forward, onto the boat, not back into the city. **Albarrán arrives**, running, in his habit, an oil lamp and Bible in one hand — he'd been at San Ignacio, praying, and felt the moment the same way the Marked did. He stops at the water's edge, looks at Mercedes, then at whichever player he's spoken to most this arc:

> _"Dios me contestó dos veces. Anoche, junto al pozo, me contestó _muévanlo_. Ahora, acá, me contestó_ hasta el otro lado del río_. Las dos respuestas son la misma. La fórmula es lo que cuesta."_

**He gives the players a beat to refuse.** If a player says _"don't,"_ he meets their eyes: _"Si no lo digo yo, lo dice ella."_ — and looks at Mercedes. **A real player choice**: if they take Mercedes' offer to bear it, Albarrán lives and Mercedes pays the full cost. If they let Albarrán speak, he stops two meters from El Patrón, looks at the water, and says first, in Spanish, the prayer his grandfather said over a drought-killed calf:

> _"Por la tierra que fue buena, y por la tierra que se cansó. Que la tierra descanse, y que nosotros pasemos ligeros."_

Then, to El Patrón, in Latin — the binding form Saráchaga taught him in 1798: _**"Hic locus est. Hic manet."**_ — _"Here is the place. Here it stays."_

> **The prayers — runner resolves.** This is the third and final utterance of _Por la tierra que fue buena_: Tomás named it at the pit-rim in C1; Don Eusebio could not finish it in [C8](chapter-08.md#scene-1--the-ritual-begins); Albarrán finishes it here. **Three speakers, two unfinished, one completed at the cost of a life.** A player who heard all three should get a private moment with the GM after the chapter. The campaign was, in part, a story of who could finish that prayer.

The geometry **dissolves** — El Patrón is no longer at the center of a door, just a man standing on a dock at dawn, unmarked, watching a boat pull away with something he's wanted for forty years. He doesn't follow onto the launch. He doesn't need to, yet — the water does something to that for him.

### Getting it aboard — the crossing

> **⏱ Short on time? Cut this whole section** — see [Pacing](#pacing--this-has-to-fit-in-3-hours-decide-the-cuts-now-not-mid-table). Nothing about Hide's cost or outcome changes: skip straight from "the geometry dissolves" to "the book crosses the river," and it's just as valid.

**The binding stops him; it doesn't stop everything else — and what's waiting depends on what hour they actually cross.**

> **At dawn (the default timing)**: the crossing is quiet. Fifty meters of open Riachuelo, proper daylight on the water, nothing surfaces. Whatever's down there stays down in the light — read the ambientación below only for texture, then go straight to "the book crosses the river." **No Deep One encounter fires at dawn.**
>
> **At night (if the players pushed to move at midnight instead — see [Dawn — departure](#dawn--departure))**: this is exactly the crossing that hour buys them. Fifty meters of open, dark Riachuelo, and something that was already down there before tonight notices a boat on it at an hour when nothing usually crosses.

> _El bote se aleja del muelle con el cofre a los pies de Borghi, los cuatro remeros clavando los remos parejo. A mitad de camino, el agua cambia — no se agita, se_ espesa_, por un tramo de unos diez metros, como si algo grande se hubiera puesto justo debajo de la superficie sin romperla. Después, cerca de la proa, una cabeza sale del agua que no es la cabeza de nadie que debería estar nadando ahí: ojos demasiado separados, sin párpados, una boca que no termina donde debería. No está sola._

**SAN Check**: 1/1D6 on first sight — a Deep One hybrid, close enough to touch the gunwale, and it is not alone; two or three more surface around the boat in the next few seconds.

**Deep One hybrids.** Not fully theirs, not fully anyone's — descendants of something that bred into a fishing settlement up the coast a long time before this table's campaign started, and never fully stopped being what it started as. Wet-skinned, wide-mouthed, strongest in the water and clumsy the instant they try to come out of it; they don't speak, but they coordinate like they don't need to. **They are not El Patrón's — nobody sent them.** He knows the river well enough after forty years to know exactly what usually stays down in the daylight, and exactly what a boat crossing it in the dark, in a hurry, on a night this disturbed, might wake up instead. Ask him and he says as much: _"Yo les dije el amanecer. Lo demás, en el río, ya no es cosa mía."_ He didn't cause this. He's not sorry about it either.

**Run as a 3-round chase, boat versus water, not a boarding fight**:
| Round | What happens | Roll |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | They test the boat — a hand on the gunwale, an oar grabbed and nearly lost. **The chest doesn't go in the water on this table**; don't dangle it as a real threat, just the people | **STR opposed or Boat Handling** to keep the launch stable; a miss costs an oar, not a life |
| 2 | One tries to climb in, or drags at a rower's leg over the side. Real melee, one exchange | **Fighting/Firearms**, one round; a rower or a PC takes a shallow bite or claw-wound, nothing crippling |
| 3 | The brig is close now — Borghi's men, alerted, fire down from the deck rail, and that's what actually breaks it off, not anything the launch does itself | — narrate the volley, the hybrids scattering back under the surface, gone as fast as they arrived |

**Borghi, once they're alongside and climbing up**: doesn't ask, doesn't want to know, crosses himself for the second time this morning and says only, to no one: _"Ese río no era así cuando yo era chico. O yo no miraba."_

**Why doesn't El Patrón just follow the boat to Montevideo?** A fair question, worth having the answer ready for if a player asks it in character. Three real reasons, not one: **(1) forty years of leverage, gone.** Everything that let him work Buenos Aires this precisely — Mercedes' stolen copy, the Lacasa bloodline's generational access, his own decades of reading this specific city's grid — is local. None of it exists in Montevideo. He'd be starting from nothing, in a city whose Keepers he's never cultivated, whose geometry he's never learned to read. **(2) he's rooted, not placeless.** Areco is where he actually is, when he isn't wearing Buenos Aires like a coat; a new country, a new river's-width away, isn't a short trip for a man whose whole method is patient, local, generational. **(3) running water, and he knows it as well as anyone.** Not an absolute wall — nothing about him is absolute — but real drag, real cost, the same folk logic every _paisano_ in this campaign already half-believes. Put together, plainly, if he says anything at all as the brig pulls out: _"Ese río, esta vez, se lo quedan ustedes."_ He means it as a small, real defeat — the only kind he's capable of admitting to.

**The book crosses the river** under Albarrán's blessing; a new vault waits for it in Montevideo, arranged in the days after through Keeper contacts Mercedes has never had to use before.

**The cost**: whoever spoke the Latin **dies within twenty-four hours**. If Albarrán, in his cell at San Ignacio, peacefully, looking at a candle: _"Dios me contestó. Era esto."_ If Mercedes, in her chair at the Manzana de las Luces, an open notebook in her lap: _"He estado esperando esto cuarenta años. Llegó tarde, pero llegó."_ And **Mercedes is permanently weakened** — she loses an eye over the next month (the cornea clouds white; painless) — _"Esto era lo justo. He estado evitándolo cuarenta años."_ **El Patrón is not destroyed** — he withdraws north, to Areco, to his other names; he will be back, not in the players' lifetimes, and not in Buenos Aires — the book isn't here to come back for anymore.

**Three costs beyond the death, all of them promised at [the vote](#the-vote), all of them due now**: **it isn't over** — a new Custodio network in Montevideo inherits the book with no warning and no say in it, the same way Mercedes did at twenty-four, and the two-hundred-and-eleven-year clock simply restarts somewhere else. **The Marks don't go quiet** — not the way they do in Held or Opened; whoever's Marked and didn't cross keeps a faint, uneven hum for the rest of their life, a low syllable at the edge of right-angled rooms that never fully resolves. And **the order fractures**: some of Casimiro's fourteen never come back to the meeting-house table after tonight, and Mercedes spends what's left of her life — however long that is — holding together an order that no longer entirely trusts the choice she pushed them toward.

The witness-stones cool; the Cabildo clock strikes the hour again, on time.

> _Imagen final:_ El bergantín _Carmen del Pilar_ se hace chico contra el agua gris, rumbo al sur. Mercedes cierra el ojo que se le va a nublar, mirándolo irse. Albarrán camina hacia San Ignacio en el primer gris del amanecer, sonriendo. El muelle vuelve a ser solamente madera. Buenos Aires se despierta y no sabe que el libro ya no está en la ciudad.

No Reader at this table — Use never opens. Not offered, not retrofitted; skip straight to the choice that's actually live.

## Option 2 — Held

> The Marked don't destroy anything and they don't hide anything. They cross. One or both of the table's Marked PCs — the Antiquarian, Negro Camba — step through the crack in the geometry, to the side of the diagram that isn't wearing a face, and spend the rest of their natural lives holding it shut from there.

**Availability**: Always, if a Marked PC wants it — no ritual, no parchment, no permission needed from Mercedes. It isn't hers to grant. It's the standing thing the Mark has been since C3: a way of perceiving the geometry that, at the absolute edge of it, becomes a way of *touching* it. Nobody in the campaign has had reason to learn this until now, including El Patrón.

**How it's offered**: not by an NPC. The crack Mercedes described — the doorframe, the far side that is "the part of him that doesn't need a face" — is right there, open, for as long as this scene runs. A Marked PC who steps toward it, hand out, doesn't need permission or a line of dialogue to justify it; the geometry already knows what they are.

> _Para el jugador que se ofrece: no hay tirada de reflejos ni de suerte. Un paso, y la grieta se abre lo justo para dejarlos pasar — no más ancha de lo que un cuerpo necesita. Del otro lado no hay nada que describir todavía; **eso es lo que lo vuelve terrible.** El aire de este lado se pone denso un segundo, como una puerta que se cierra de golpe en otro cuarto, y listo._

**If both cross**: they hold it together — less alone, and by every account in Mercedes' old fragments, a door held by two lasts longer than a door held by one. Run the epilogue warmer for it.
**If only one crosses**: the other lives, here, with what that means for the rest of their life. Run the epilogue quieter, and let that PC's player decide how they carry it.
**If neither crosses**: Held simply isn't taken. Hide is still there — and if that isn't taken either, [Opened](#option-3--opened) is what's actually happening, not a pause.

**The cost**: not death. **They live out the whole of their natural life — however long that would have been anyway — on the other side, spending it holding the door shut.** No SAN drain for the years themselves (they're not described; nobody at the table has to live them), just the one **SAN Check: 1/1D6** for the act of crossing, and the surviving PC's chair at the table, from here on, is simply empty. **El Patrón is not destroyed and not banished from existing — he is denied this specific door, absolutely, for as long as the volunteer(s) hold it.** Whether that's forty years or four, the campaign doesn't get to know; neither does he.

**El Patrón's reaction — the one moment his composure actually breaks**, however briefly: whatever wore that handprint into the doorframe did it long before his forty years, maybe long before Buenos Aires had a name — an old road, not a new one, but one he never thought anyone left standing would still know how to walk. _"Eso—"_ un silencio real, el primero de la mañana — _"—no lo esperaba de ustedes. De alguien, sí. No de ustedes."_ He recovers in a breath, smiles again, bows: but a player who's spent four arcs watching him be unbeatable should get to see it crack, once, here.

> _Imagen final:_ La grieta se cierra sobre la mano del último Marcado en cruzar. Del lado de acá, nada cambia visiblemente — el muelle sigue siendo madera, el amanecer sigue llegando. Del lado de allá, no hay imagen que dar, porque nadie que haya cruzado volvió jamás a describirlo. Mercedes se queda mirando el punto exacto donde la grieta estuvo, durante mucho más tiempo del que debería.

**Combining with Hide**: nothing stops a table from doing both — the book still crosses to Montevideo, and a Marked PC still steps through, on the same morning. There's no mechanical bonus for it and no penalty either; it just means the table wanted both kinds of ending, and can have them.

## Option 3 — Opened

> Nobody hides it. Nobody crosses. The chest opens inside the diagram — by his hand, by a player's, or simply because the sixty seconds ran out and nobody stopped it — and the geometry does what it was always going to do if left alone: it calls the rest of him through.

**This is the default, not a special case.** If Mercedes' sixty seconds run out with the table still arguing, if a player opens the chest for him, hands it over, or simply doesn't act — this is what happens. It isn't a failure state to apologize for; it's the campaign's third real ending, and for some tables it's the truest one: four arcs of being managed, outmaneuvered, and out-waited by something patient enough to spend forty years on this, and in the end he simply wins.

**What happens**: no violence, no gloating. El Patrón takes the chest — handed to him, or simply reached for while nobody stops him — and opens it, right there, inside the closed geometry. What comes through isn't a monster stepping onto the dock; it's the rest of him, the mass of the avatar that has never needed the face he's worn since 1798, filling in behind his eyes in the space of one breath. For a moment he looks like a man remembering something enormous. Then he looks like a man again, and thanks them.

> _"Cuarenta años. Gracias — de verdad — por terminarlo. No hacía falta que lo entendieran para hacerlo. Casi nadie lo entiende nunca."_

**The cost**: no tiers, no single volunteer — this one lands on everyone evenly, because nobody chose to pay anything. **SAN Check: 1/1D10** — not gore, but the particular horror of watching four arcs of effort resolve into nothing, in complete quiet, with no violence afterward to even be angry at. **The Marks go out** — not quiet, not transferred, just gone mid-beat, like a sound that had been running under everything since C3 finally finishing. **El Patrón takes the book and walks away**, unhurried, up the street or up the stairs, and nobody follows, because there is nothing left in the scene that could stop him now.

**Buenos Aires still wakes up and doesn't know** — same as every other ending. That's this one's particular cruelty: nothing about the city changes today. What changes is slower, larger, and entirely off-page — a hook for whatever comes after this campaign, not a summary to write now. Close the door on this session; leave the sequel unwritten.

> _Imagen final:_ El Patrón se aleja calle arriba, o escalera arriba, sin apuro, el cofre bajo el brazo como quien vuelve del mercado. Nadie lo sigue. El amanecer sigue llegando, igual que en cualquier otro final. Buenos Aires no sabe. Esta vez, tampoco lo va a saber nunca.

## Live at the table — keep this short

**Run only this much live, then close the session.** The rest is a written epilogue (below) sent afterward — the table's last minutes should stay on the weight of the Choice, not turn into a checklist.

> **The dust lifts** — the campaign-spanning dust runner resolves here, at the geometry's completion (**every ending, Opened included** — the working finishes either way, only who it finishes _for_ changes). Ten months of fine pale grit on cuffs, seams, hatbands — gone in one beat: _en el segundo posterior a que se cierra el trabajo, el polvo **se levanta**. No cae; sube en una sola capa entera, queda suspendido un latido, y desaparece — como si la geometría a la que estaba pegado por fin lo hubiera soltado._ The Marked feel it in their cuffs first: _"Se fue."_ **Show it to the Marked PCs and let them name what they see. In Hide or Held, let the relief land. In Opened, let the same physical sensation land as wrong instead — a comfort with no right to be there.**

Close on the dawn image: _el amanecer llega despacio sobre el Riachuelo (o sobre la Manzana); el muelle, o la calle, vuelve a ser solamente lo que era; el olor a sebo y sal, o a piedra fría, sigue igual que siempre — eso, más que cualquier otra cosa, es lo que convence de que la noche terminó, para bien o para mal._

## Written epilogue — send after the session, not run live

*(Everything from here down is prose to paste/send once the table breaks, not table time.)*

### The rest of the survivors

**This round assumes Hide or Held.** If Opened is what happened, skip it — there is no round of survivor epilogues, because nothing has settled enough yet for anyone to have a line. Go straight to [the land](#the-land) below, Opened's version.

- **Albarrán** (if he survived — not the Hide ending): _"Voy a confesarme cuando llegue a San Ignacio, y voy a confesar nada, porque no hay palabras para esto. Dios va a entender."_
- **Ana Bermúdez** — *at this table, still unresolved*: never confronted, still writing to Quirce, still not knowing what the players know about her. Whether the Network learns anything about how this morning ended depends entirely on what she chooses to send him next, off-page — a loose thread you can pick back up if you ever want El Patrón's network to have a next move.
- **Quirce** (if encountered): _"Observé desde lejos. No sé bien qué pasó esta mañana, y eso es nuevo para mí. Mi Red va a querer saberlo. Voy a averiguarlo, con o sin Ana."_
- **Héctor / Marta** (if alive): they continue fading out of geometric alignment over the months; see the [C9 epilogues](chapter-09.md#the-cost).
- **Inocencia**: keeps her boarding house, does not remember El Patrón, remembers the players fondly and — oddly clearly — _the bread on the table_ the night before, which she tells her grandchildren about for the rest of her life.

### The land

**Hide or Held** — the peaceful version, things visibly settling:

> _La estancia La Esperanza está tranquila — el ganado del pastizal sur vuelve a morirse, pero normalmente; los anillos de pasto se borraron. San Ruiz se repuebla despacio: la capilla con techo nuevo de chapa, Rosa cultivando maíz en lo que fue el pozo, el aljibe vacío donde antes estaba la cabra. Las ruinas jesuíticas siguen en ruinas — la higuera, inexplicablemente, da fruto este año por primera vez, y dos chicos de un paraje lo juntan. La cordillera está en silencio; el Zonda llegó tarde y sin nada de particular; las langostas no volvieron. Buenos Aires se despertó a la mañana siguiente y no supo. La Universidad abrió a las ocho; los estudiantes discutían gramática latina._

**Opened** — nothing settles, it just goes quiet in a way that isn't the same thing:

> _La estancia La Esperanza sigue tranquila, pero Tomás deja de dormir bien esa semana sin saber por qué. San Ruiz no se repuebla ni empeora — se queda exactamente como estaba, y Rosa, que llevaba años sin rezar en voz alta, vuelve a hacerlo, sola, sin decirle a nadie por qué ahora. Las ruinas jesuíticas no cambian. La cordillera no dice nada, lo cual, por primera vez en la campaña, es lo que más inquieta a quien todavía sabe leerla. Buenos Aires se despertó a la mañana siguiente y no supo — pero esa frase, esta vez, no es un alivio. La Universidad abrió a las ocho; los estudiantes discutían gramática latina, y en alguna oficina de la Manzana, sin que nadie lo note todavía, un solo libro de la biblioteca no está donde el catálogo dice que debería estar. No lo van a notar por años._

### The final image

> _La Luz Mala parpadea, una vez, al borde del cuadro — sobre el pozo, las ruinas jesuíticas, o el muelle, según el final. Queda colgada dos segundos. Después —_
> 
> - _**[Hidden]** desciende a la tierra sin mover el suelo — acá, o del otro lado de un río, ya no importa cuál._
> - _**[Held]** no desciende. Se queda exactamente donde está, colgada, más tiempo del que debería — como esperando a alguien que no va a volver a mirarla desde este lado._
> - _**[Opened]** cambia de color, una sola vez, a uno que nadie en el cuadro sabe nombrar. Después sigue ahí, igual que siempre — solo que ahora nadie que la mire vuelve a estar seguro de qué color era antes._

> **The annual dream** _(between-chapter hook 5d — run once per in-fiction year, if the epilogue is kept alive)_: On the calendar night of the closing, one surviving investigator dreams of the pit — only the pit, at La Esperanza, the night the closing happened. They stand at its rim; the bowl is full of short green ordinary grass; **Concepción is at the center**, fourteen then fifteen then sixteen, humming a different tune every year. She does not look up. They wake calmly, will not remember the dream by afternoon, but write the date in their journal every year without remembering why. Rotate which PC dreams; none dream it twice in the same year. _(Long-form: run it ten years — year three wildflowers, year five children playing in the bowl, year seven a small chapel built by Rosa's nephews for Dolores, year ten just **land**, no longer remembering itself. The dream stops; the campaign is finally, fully over.)_
