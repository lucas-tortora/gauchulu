# EL QUE ESPERA EN EL ALTO

*One-shot — Call of Cthulhu 7e, modo Pulp · Buenos Aires y la campaña del sur, 1827 · 3 horas · **2 investigadores** (+ aliados reclutables)*

*A port of "Edge of Darkness" to the Río de la Plata under the Brazilian war. Same-world, light tie-in to La Tierra Mala: same pampa, same register and voseo (see `assets/spanish-style-guide.md`), **no plot connection**. Sister one-shots: `../lo-que-se-agarra-del-subterraneo/` and `../la-casa-de-la-calle-chile/` — same house rules, same series feel.*

---

## CÓMO USAR ESTE DOCUMENTO

- **Read-aloud** blocks are in Spanish, blockquoted, period-register. Read them straight off the page.
- **Keeper notes** are in English, plain text. Italic lines with an em-dash are glosses **for you** — don't read them out.
- **Modo Pulp, house rules for tonight**:
  - **HP ×2** on both investigator sheets.
  - **Bennies**: once per scene, each investigator may auto-succeed a failed roll by narrating how. Keeper's call on whether the fiction earns it.
  - **Pulp talents**, one each: *Duro de matar* (regain 1D6 HP once/session, free action) · *Puntería* (one Firearms roll/session is an automatic Hard success) · *Nervios de acero* (ignore one SAN loss/session entirely) · *Manos rápidas* (act first in any round you'd be surprised).
  - **Locura abreviada**: bouts of madness last one round, not 1D10. With two players you cannot afford to bench one.
- **The big structural change is [MESA DE DOS](#mesa-de-dos--cómo-se-ajusta-todo).** Read that before anything else. Everything downstream is built on it.
- **Versión de tres horas.** The investigation stage (Acto II, and the research half of Acto III) is compressed. **La Biblioteca is cut by default.** Most of what the players used to have to dig for — the Cofradía's history, and whatever they're most directly on the trail of at the time (la Malena, the pattern of deaths, the nature of the thing) — is now handed to them straight, on the page or out of an NPC's mouth, with no roll standing between them and it. The time that frees up goes to the recruiting scenes and the siege, which is what the scenario is actually about.

## MESA DE DOS — CÓMO SE AJUSTA TODO

*The original scenario assumes four to six investigators, because the closing ritual is explicitly a group activity: more chanters, faster ritual. Two players break that math outright — two chanters would need the full two hours while simultaneously fighting off a siege, and would burn nearly every magic point they own doing it.*

**Four changes fix it. Use all four.**

### 1 — El canto se puede partir
**At any given moment the ritual needs exactly one voice.** It does not need both. It does not need everyone. If a single person is chanting, the ritual is alive and the clock is running; the instant nobody is chanting, it stops, and if it stays stopped for more than **one round** it collapses and cannot be restarted until the next midnight.

This is the whole tactical shape of the finale for a two-player table: **one chants, one fights, and they swap.** Say this out loud to the players before midnight so they can plan around it. Swapping is a free action on either character's turn.

### 2 — Aliados reclutables — *la parte principal del arreglo*
**Three NPCs can be recruited during the investigation and taught the chant.** Each is winnable, each has a real condition, and **each one shortens the ritual**. This is the two-player table's actual objective in the middle act — not "find clues," but *find people brave enough to sit in that house at midnight*.

| Aliado | Dónde | Vale | El costo |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ña Petrona Saldívar** | La pulpería, El Rincón | **1 cantor** — reliable, will not run | Must be convinced the thing took la Malena. She's the achievable one. |
| **El Colorado** (Santiago Barreto) | El casco, escondido | **1 cantor** + the best pair of hands in the fight | Only if they don't hand him to the *leva*. **If he dies he comes back**, and they will have known him. |
| **El padre Timoteo** | La capilla, El Rincón | **1½ cantores** — he reads Latin properly | The hardest. Twenty years of refusing to go near that house. |

**Latin, and why the priest is worth so much**: Mariano Alén wrote the chant out **twice** — once in Latin, once transliterated into Spanish spelling for the brothers who had no letters. Anyone can chant from the transliteration, so **no investigator is locked out by their sheet**. But a fluent Latin reader doesn't stumble, and stumbling costs time: a Latin reader counts as **1½ chanters**.

### 3 — El reloj del ritual, reescalado

| Cantores | Duración | Termina (empezando a medianoche) |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | 150 min | 02:30 — effectively impossible; don't let them try it alone unless they insist |
| **2** *(los dos PJs solos)* | **120 min** | **02:00** |
| **3** | 95 min | 01:35 |
| **4** | 75 min | 01:15 |
| **4½** *(3 + el padre)* | 65 min | 01:05 |
| **5+** | 55 min | 00:55 — floor, it does not go faster than this |

**Coste**: **1 magic point per 30 minutes per chanter** (halved from the original for pulp mode). Two PCs running the full 120 minutes pay **4 MP each** — real, affordable, and it means a POW 50 investigator ends the night nearly dry, which is exactly the feeling wanted. If a chanter has no MP left, they pay **1 HP per 30 minutes** instead and keep going.

**The pitch to the players**: every ally recruited cuts roughly twenty minutes off the worst night of their lives. Make sure they understand that in the middle act, and the middle act runs itself.

### 4 — El asedio, reducido
The siege timeline below has a **two-player column**. Fewer attackers, staggered rather than concurrent, and the *vizcachas* demoted from an encounter to a noise. Do not run the full-table siege against two people; it isn't a challenge, it's a mugging.

**And give them the powder.** The **polvo de Ibn-Ghazi** (three throws) is the difference between a fight and a slaughter, and a two-player table should be told plainly what it does before midnight — by Rufino, by the papers, by Petrona. Withholding that information is not suspense, it's a design failure.

## LA VERDAD DEL KEEPER

*(Not for players.)*

In **1783**, six students of the newly founded **Real Colegio de San Carlos** in Buenos Aires formed a society they called, without irony, **la Cofradía de la Puerta**. They were rich, bored, well-read in the wrong languages, and led by the cleverest and worst of them: **Mariano Alén**.

Alén's family owned an *estancia* on the southern post road, three days out from the city, and the six of them spent a summer at its *casco* doing what such boys do — séances, invocations, a Latin book bought off a Genoese sailor at the docks together with **a stone coffin the sailor swore came out of Egypt.** For most of that summer nothing happened.

On a night in **February 1784**, something answered.

They did not summon it into the sarcophagus. They summoned it into **the loft of the house**, and then they discovered they could not put it back. What they *could* do — what Alén found, in the Latin book, after three weeks of not sleeping — was **bind it to the house**. Not banish. Bind. A working that held only so long as **the man who cast it drew breath**.

Alén cast it. The six of them rode back to the city and never spoke of it again in writing.

Over forty-four years the Cofradía dispersed and died off. **Mariano Alén was murdered in Montevideo in 1819** — his heart cut out and marks carved into his face — and the binding did not break, because Alén had been clever enough not to be the caster. He made the youngest and most frightened of them do it.

That was **Rufino Miraval**, then seventeen. He is now sixty-one, and he has a tumour in his lung, and he has perhaps a week.

**When Rufino dies, the thing in the loft is free.** It has been in that loft for forty-four years with nothing to do. It can already reach a little way past the walls — enough to kill stock, enough to take **Magdalena Quiroga** three weeks ago, enough to make the whole district avoid the road past the casco after dark. Free, it will not stay in the pampa. Rufino knows this. Rufino has known it since he was seventeen and has done nothing about it for forty-four years, and that is the fact he is dying of, more than the tumour.

**What the players are being asked to do is not what Alén did.** Rufino is not asking them to re-bind it — a binding needs a caster and a caster's whole remaining life. He is asking them to **put it in the box**: the banishment Alén found in the same Latin book and never had the courage to attempt, because the banishment has to be performed **inside the house, at night, with the thing awake and aware of exactly what you are doing.**

**Historical texture, free**: it is 1827. The war with Brazil is in its third year, the port is blockaded, and the *leva* — the press-gang — has been through the southern campaña twice this year taking every man without a work-pass. **The countryside is empty of young men.** That is why the district is depopulated, why the posta is short-handed, why there are so few people to recruit, and why **el Colorado** is hiding in a haunted house rather than sleeping in a village like a sane person.

## LOS INVESTIGADORES

Written for **two**. Default framing: two men of the campaña — a *gaucho* and whatever the second player wants — who owe **don Rufino Miraval** something. Pick one at character creation and make it concrete:

- He bought your father's debt and then tore it up.
- He testified for you once, in a matter of a knife and a dead man, and he did not have to.
- You worked his *estancia* for nine years and he never once shorted you.
- He is your godfather and you have not visited in six years and you are about to find out why that matters.

**Two is a small party. Compensate deliberately**: both should have something the other doesn't, and between them they should cover *at least* one social skill (Persuade/Fast Talk/Charm), one physical skill (Fighting/Firearms), and one head skill (Spot Hidden/Occult/Library Use). If the players build two identical knife-fighters, the middle act will grind. Say so at character creation.

## ACTO I — EL LECHO DE DON RUFINO

*Ten to fifteen minutes. Buenos Aires, a shuttered bedroom on calle Venezuela, a Thursday, one in the afternoon.*

> *La casa es buena y está en penumbra a las dos de la tarde, con las persianas bajas y un olor a alcanfor que no alcanza a tapar el otro olor. En la sala hay una mujer de sesenta y pico sentada muy derecha, con las manos cruzadas, que no llora porque hace tres semanas que se le acabó. Y hay un hombre de unos treinta y cinco, bien vestido, parado junto a la ventana, mirando la calle como quien espera que esto termine para poder almorzar.*
>
> *Los hacen pasar al cuarto del fondo. Don Rufino Miraval tiene sesenta y un años y parece que tuviera ochenta. Está sentado contra tres almohadas porque acostado se ahoga. Cuando tose — y tose — se aparta el pañuelo de la boca rápido, para que ustedes no vean, y ustedes ven.*

### DOÑA ÁGATA MIRAVAL — *la esposa*
Sixty-four. Composed to the point of brittleness. She knows **nothing** about the Cofradía, the casco, or any of it, and forty-four years of marriage to a man with a locked box is sitting behind her eyes.

> *—Él los pidió a ustedes. No a los médicos, no al cura, no a su hijo. A ustedes. Yo no voy a preguntar por qué, porque hace cuarenta años que no pregunto y no voy a empezar ahora, faltando tan poco. Pero si lo van a hacer llorar, no entren.*

**Si sale bien la noche** — she is the person to whom the ending is reported, and she takes it better than anyone expects, because she has been waiting four decades for somebody to finally say a true sentence in that house.

### BERNABÉ MIRAVAL — *el hijo*
Thirty-six, well-tailored, entirely indifferent. He wants the estate settled and he considers his father's summoning of two men from the campaña to be an embarrassing final eccentricity.

> *—Miren, sin ofender. Mi padre está muy enfermo y a esta altura dice cosas. Si les prometió algo, hablen conmigo y lo arreglamos como gente. Si les prometió campo, les aviso desde ya que ese campo del sur no vale el papel de la escritura y hace cuarenta años que no se pisa.*

**Key Hook**: Bernabé is not a villain and is not in on anything. He is the scenario's one purely mundane obstacle and his function is to be *tiresome* — he will try to buy the deed back, he will follow up afterwards, and if things end badly **he is the one who goes to the juez de paz**. Play him as reasonable, which is worse.

### DON RUFINO MIRAVAL — *el moribundo*

**Key Hook**: he is not mysterious. He is a frightened old man making a confession forty-four years late, and he will answer **every** question honestly and in order, because he has been rehearsing this conversation since 1784 and does not have time for coyness. **Do not make the players roll for information here.** This scene is a gift of exposition; the difficulty is emotional, not procedural.

| STR | CON | SIZ | DEX | INT | POW | EDU | APP | HP | SAN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25 | 15 | 55 | 30 | 80 | 65 | 75 | 30 | 7 | 40 |

**Skills**: Occult 55%, Latin 60%, History 50%, Persuade 60%, Cthulhu Mythos 8%

> *—Gracias por venir. No los voy a hacer perder tiempo con cortesías porque no me sobra. Les voy a decir una cosa y quiero que la escuchen entera antes de contestar.*
>
> *—Cuando yo me muera — esta semana, la que viene, no más — se va a soltar una cosa que está encerrada desde el año ochenta y cuatro. La encerré yo. Tenía diecisiete años y no sabía lo que hacía, y el que sí sabía me puso a mí a hacerlo porque yo era el más chico y el más asustado y no me iba a negar.*
>
> *—No les estoy pidiendo que me crean. Les estoy pidiendo que vayan y miren, y si lo que ven no les parece nada, se vuelven, se quedan con lo que hay en la caja y con el campo, y yo me muero igual y ustedes no perdieron más que un viaje.*

**Sus respuestas** — he has them all ready:

**¿Qué es?**
> *—No sé. Cuarenta y cuatro años preguntándomelo y no sé. Vive en el alto de la casa, arriba del cielorraso, y no baja porque no puede, y no puede porque yo dije unas palabras en latín cuando tenía diecisiete años. Mata animales. Se llevó a una mujer del Rincón, hace tres semanas — eso lo supe por carta y no dormí desde entonces. Y usa a los muertos como uno usa un guante.*

**¿Por qué nosotros?**
> *—Porque no tengo a nadie más. Mi hijo cree que estoy delirando. Los otros cinco están muertos. El cura de acá me da la extremaunción y me palmea la mano. Y ustedes... ustedes me deben algo, y yo estoy tan bajo que lo voy a usar. Perdónenme eso.*

**¿No se puede volver a encerrar?**
> *—Sí. Le cuesta a alguien lo que me costó a mí: la vida entera, hasta el último día, sabiendo que el día que se muera se suelta. No se lo voy a pedir a nadie. Alén me lo pidió a mí y no se lo perdoné nunca.*

**Entonces, ¿qué?**
> *—Meterlo en la caja. Está todo en los papeles de Mariano, en el sótano del casco. Él encontró cómo, y nunca se animó, porque hay que hacerlo adentro de la casa, de noche, con la cosa despierta y mirando. Yo tampoco me animé. Cuarenta y cuatro años.* *(Se queda callado.)* *—Ustedes decidan mejor que yo.*

**Si le preguntan por Mariano Alén:**
> *—Lo mataron en Montevideo, en el diecinueve. Le sacaron el corazón y le tallaron unos dibujos en la cara. La familia dijo que fue la guerra. No fue la guerra. Mariano tenía tratos con más gente que nosotros seis, y algunos de esos tratos le sobrevivieron.*
>
> **GM**: a door you never open. Nobody from that thread appears tonight. Leave it ajar.

**LA CAJA.** He has Ágata bring a *baúl* from under the bed. Inside:

1. **La escritura del campo y la llave del casco** — the deed to the Alén property, bought by Rufino in 1801 for exactly this reason, made over to the investigators in a paper already signed and witnessed. **The property is legally theirs the moment they take it.** (This matters: it makes them the owners in any later legal trouble, and it is also the only payment.)
2. **La carta de Rufino** — **HANDOUT 1**, to be read on the road, not here.
3. **El arcón de piedra** — the sarcophagus. Man-length, dark stone, far too heavy for two men to move without a cart; getting it to the casco is a logistical problem the players should have to solve. Empty. The **exterior** is unmistakably Egyptian — a stylised face, a beard, columns of small figures. The **interior** is not. The interior is carved with tight rows of pictographs that no scholar in Buenos Aires can read, and that a **History or Archaeology (Hard)** success identifies as **older than Egypt and not from anywhere Egypt traded with.** SAN 0/1 for anyone who thinks about that too hard.
4. **El diario de Rufino, 1783–1789** — a young man's diary that starts silly and stops being silly on a specific page. Reading it: **SAN 1D2, Cthulhu Mythos +1%.**

**Si aceptan**, Rufino does one more thing:

> *Les agarra la mano — la mano, no el brazo — con las dos suyas, que no pesan nada.*
>
> *—Una cosa más y no los molesto más. Si no se puede, si llegan y ven que no se puede: váyanse. Váyanse de veras, no se hagan los valientes por un viejo. Yo ya arruiné a un muchacho de diecisiete años con esto. Uno me alcanza.*

**Si preguntan cuánto tiempo tienen**: the *médico* says a week. The médico is being kind. **Rufino has four days**, and the Keeper should let the players feel the clock without ever stating it as a number.

## HANDOUT 1 — LA CARTA DE DON RUFINO MIRAVAL

> *Si están leyendo esto, ya salieron de la ciudad y ya no puedo hablarles. Escribo lo que no fui capaz de decirles a la cara.*
>
> *Éramos seis en el Colegio de San Carlos, el año ochenta y tres. Mariano Alén, que era el que mandaba; Casimiro Ávalos; los hermanos Lastra; Fermín Oyuela; y yo, que era el más chico. Nos decíamos la Cofradía de la Puerta y nos creíamos muy sabios y en verdad no éramos más que seis muchachos con plata y con un libro que no debimos comprar.*
>

>
> *No se pudo sacar. Se pudo atar. En el libro estaba cómo, y hacía falta uno que lo dijera y que lo sostuviera con la vida, y Mariano me eligió a mí. Yo tenía diecisiete años. Le dije que sí porque me daba más miedo decirle que no a Mariano que decírselo a la cosa del alto.*
>
> *Cuarenta y cuatro años. Me casé, tuve un hijo, hice dinero, fui a misa, y todas las noches de esos cuarenta y cuatro años me acosté sabiendo que el día que yo me muriera se soltaba. No se lo dije a Ágata. No se lo dije a nadie. Esa es la clase de hombre que resulté ser y ya no tengo tiempo de ser otro.*
>
> *En el sótano del casco está el baúl de Mariano. Ahí están los polvos y ahí están los papeles con lo que él encontró y nunca hizo. Léanlos. Si se puede, háganlo. Si no se puede, quémenlo todo y váyanse y no me deban nada, que la deuda era mía.*
>
> *Perdónenme el pedido. No tengo con qué pagarlo.*
>
> *— Rufino Miraval*

## ACTO II — EL CAMINO Y EL RINCÓN

*Three days south by *galera* or on their own horses. Budget twenty-five to thirty minutes of table time for the village. **This is where they recruit chanters — that is the act's job.** Everything else is texture, and in this cut, texture is given, not dug for: don't make the players roll to learn what they need to know, just tell them and spend the saved time on the recruiting conversations, which are the point.*

### EL RINCÓN

> *El Rincón es una posta, una pulpería, una capilla de adobe con una campana que no tiene badajo, y catorce ranchos de los cuales seis están cerrados con tablas. Cuarenta almas, dice el padrón. El padrón es del año pasado y el padrón miente, porque la leva pasó dos veces este año y se llevó a todos los hombres que no tenían papeleta de conchabo.*
>
> *Lo que queda son mujeres, viejos, chicos, y tres o cuatro hombres con papeles en regla que miran el camino todo el día por si vuelve la partida.*

**Establish immediately**: this place is emptied out and frightened of the *road*, not of ghosts. The supernatural is a distant second on their list of problems, which makes it much more convincing when they get to it anyway.

### ÑA PETRONA SALDÍVAR — *la pulpera* · **ALIADA #1**

Sixty, wide, entirely unbothered, has run the pulpería alone since her husband died and considers most human problems to be a matter of somebody needing to sit down and drink something. She is the closest thing El Rincón has to a government.

**Key Hook**: she is publicly dismissive of the casco and privately not. She has heard forty years of stories and she believes maybe two of them — but she believes **those two**, and one of them is about la Malena.

| STR | CON | SIZ | DEX | INT | POW | EDU | APP | HP | DB | Build | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 60 | 70 | 70 | 45 | 70 | 75 | 40 | 45 | 14 | +1D4 | 1 | 6 |

**Skills**: Persuade 65%, Bargain 75%, Listen 70%, Spot Hidden 60%, Natural World 55%, First Aid 50%, Fighting (Brawl) 45%, **Latin (liturgical, by rote) 25%**

**Weapons**: *un trabuco* behind the counter, 40% (2D6/1D6/1 blunderbuss spread), loaded since the first *leva* came through.

**Diálogos — Ña Petrona**

**Si preguntan por el casco:**
> *—¿La casa del alto? Está a legua y media por el camino viejo, para el norte, y se llega solo. No tiene pérdida porque no hay otra cosa.* *(Se seca las manos en el delantal.)* *—Y no, no está embrujada, y sí, todo el mundo acá le da la vuelta, y las dos cosas son ciertas y no se contradicen, y no me pregunte cómo porque hace treinta años que vivo con eso.*

**Si preguntan por la gente que desapareció:**
> *(Deja de secarse las manos.)*
>
> *—Magdalena Quiroga. La Malena. Treinta y dos años, dos chicos, buena mujer. Salió a buscar una vaca que se le había ido para el norte, hace tres semanas, un jueves a la tarde. Salió a las cuatro y a las seis ya la estábamos buscando, y la buscamos nueve días.*
>
> *—Encontramos la vaca. A doscientos pasos del casco, abierta, con el pecho abierto y sin corazón. A ella no.*

**SAN 0/1.** **This is the lever for recruiting her.** Anyone who connects the casco to la Malena in front of her — with the diary, with Rufino's letter, with the dead stock — has her.

**El reclutamiento (no roll if they bring evidence; Persuade Regular if they only bring conviction):**
> *—A ver. Yo tengo sesenta años, no tengo marido, no tengo hijos y tengo un negocio que si me muero lo agarra mi sobrino, que es un inútil pero es de la familia. Si ustedes me están diciendo que esa casa se llevó a la Malena y que hay una forma de terminarlo, yo cierro la pulpería y voy.*
>
> *—Ahora: yo rezo el rosario en latín desde los seis años. Latín de verdad no sé, pero decir palabras raras despacio y sin equivocarme, eso lo hago mejor que ustedes dos juntos. Denme el papel y díganme dónde me siento.*

**Si la rechazan o la dejan afuera "por su seguridad":** she comes anyway, arrives at 00:40, and walks in through the front door in the middle of the siege, which is a *fantastic* beat — but she arrives **after** the clock started, so she only reduces the remaining time from the moment she sits down. Let them feel the cost of having been chivalrous.

### EL PADRE TIMOTEO — *el cura* · **ALIADO #2** (el difícil)

Seventy-one, half-deaf, has held the chapel at El Rincón for twenty-six years. He is not a fraud, he is not corrupt, and he is not brave. **He has known there is something wrong with that house since 1806** and his entire pastoral strategy has been to tell people not to go there and to change the subject.

**Key Hook**: he is ashamed, and he has been ashamed for twenty years, and shame is the only handle on him. He cannot be persuaded that the house is dangerous — he already knows. He can only be persuaded that **this time somebody is actually going to do something and he can be part of it.**

| STR | CON | SIZ | DEX | INT | POW | EDU | APP | HP | Move |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 45 | 55 | 35 | 70 | 60 | 70 | 45 | 10 | 5 |

**Skills**: **Language (Latin) 70%**, History 45%, Persuade 40%, Occult 30%, Listen 25% *(he is genuinely hard of hearing — play it)*

**El reclutamiento — dos pasos, y no se saltea ninguno:**

**Paso 1 — hacerlo admitir que sabe.** He denies, deflects, offers *mate*, asks about the city. Requires **Psychology (Regular)** to see the deflection for what it is, then a direct, unkind, accurate question. There is no roll for the question — the player has to actually ask it.

> *(Cuando por fin deja de esquivar, no se defiende. Se saca los lentes y se los limpia con la sotana, que es lo que hace cuando no quiere que le vean los ojos.)*
>
> *—En el año seis vino un peón a buscarme. Me dijo que había ruido arriba del cielorraso del casco y que él no volvía a entrar. Yo agarré el hisopo y el ritual y monté y llegué hasta el palenque.*
>
> *—Y no me bajé del caballo. Recé desde arriba del caballo, a treinta varas de la puerta, y me volví, y le dije al peón que ya estaba, que quedara con Dios. Eso hice. Veintiún años hace.*

**SAN 0/1** — not for horror. For recognising it.

**Paso 2 — darle algo que hacer, no algo que creer.** He will not be argued into faith in a Mythos banishment. He will absolutely be recruited by *"padre, hay un papel en latín que hay que leer bien y no hay nadie acá que lo lea bien."* **No roll** if a player approaches it that way. **Hard Persuade** for anything else. Intimidation, mockery, or theological argument fails automatically and costs him for good.

> *—Yo no sé lo que es esa cosa y no me lo expliquen, porque si me lo explican voy a tener que decidir qué pienso y no tengo tiempo. Ustedes necesitan a alguien que lea latín sin trabarse. Eso es lo único que yo sé hacer bien en este mundo. Voy.*
>
> *—Una condición: no me pidan que lo bendiga ni que lo exorcice ni nada de eso, porque no me corresponde y porque no funcionaría y los dos lo sabemos. Yo leo. Es todo lo que traigo.*

**Vale 1½ cantores.** He is also **the most likely ally to die**, and if he does it should be while still reading — he does not stop, and that is the whole point of him.

### EL CASCO Y EL COLORADO — *ver [Acto III](#acto-iii--el-casco)*

The third recruitable ally is **not** in the village. He is squatting in the haunted house, which the players will discover the hard way.

### OTRAS COSAS EN EL RINCÓN

- **La capilla** has a **libro de difuntos** going back to 1791. **No roll — this is handed to them, not found.** Padre Timoteo shows it himself if approached (or Ña Petrona describes it, unprompted, if he wasn't): over thirty-six years, **eleven** deaths and disappearances recorded within a league of the casco — a rate about four times the rest of the parish. Padre Timoteo's own hand wrote nine of them and never once wrote *why*.
- **La posta** — the relay station. The postmaster will rent horses, a cart for the sarcophagus, and will absolutely not go north on the old road after dark, at any price, and does not consider this worth discussing.
- **Ambiente**: every evening at dusk the remaining families bring their stock *inside the ring of ranchos*. Nobody explains this. Nobody has explained it in years. It is simply what you do at El Rincón.

*Cut this scene. **La Biblioteca Pública**, in the Manzana de las Luces, and the six-year-old **Universidad**.

**EL DOCTOR HILARIÓN ORZÁBAL** — sixty, dry, guards the *armario cerrado* (the locked cabinet) the way other men guard money. He is not obstructive on principle; he is obstructive because the last man he lent something to did not bring it back.

## ACTO III — EL CASCO

*The house. Arrive in daylight. Budget forty minutes.*

> *Legua y media de camino viejo, con el pasto crecido en el medio de las huellas porque hace años que no pasa una carreta. Y después, en un alto suave, el casco: una casa larga y baja, de las de antes, con paredes de ladrillo de una vara de grueso y techo de teja a dos aguas. Fue linda. Tuvo parral, tuvo higuera, tuvo un palenque de quebracho que todavía está parado porque el quebracho no se cae.*

### EL CAMPO ALREDEDOR

Walking the grounds — **no roll, it is not hidden**:

> *Alrededor de la casa, en un círculo de unas doscientas varas, está lo que quedó de los animales. Una vaca. Dos ovejas. Un perro. Un potrillo. Están secos, tirados, en distintos grados de haberse ido, y todos, todos sin excepción, tienen el pecho abierto y les falta lo mismo.*

**SAN 0/1D3.**

**No roll — say it straight.** The wounds were not made by teeth or by a knife. Something opened these animals **from the inside of the ribcage outward**, which is not a direction from which anything can reach.

**Anyone who counts** finds that the kills get **more recent as you get closer to the house**, and the freshest is four days old, twenty paces from the door.

### EL CASCO — PLANTA

A long, low, single-storey house, six rooms in a row opening onto a *galería*, a **sótano** beneath the two western rooms, and, above the *cielorraso* (the flat cane-and-plaster ceiling) under the tiled roof, **el alto** — a crawl-height loft running the entire length of the building.

- **La galería** — the covered porch. Roof intact. The *only* part of the house that feels like a house.
- **La sala y el comedor** — furniture still here, ruined by forty years of damp and mice. **The big room is where the ritual happens** — establish this early, and let the players choose it themselves by pointing out it's the only room with a clear floor and one door.
- **Los dormitorios** — three, empty, the beds stripped long ago by whoever came through first.
- **La cocina** — an enormous fogón and a chimney a man could climb. Remember the chimney.
- **El sótano** — under the west end. Cool store, brick vaulted, reached by a trapdoor and eight steps. **Mariano's trunk is here.**
- **EL ALTO** — the loft. **It lives here.** There is one hatch, in the ceiling of the back corridor, and it is nailed shut, and the nails are on the *underside*.

### **EL COLORADO** — SANTIAGO BARRETO · **ALIADO #3**

*The players will find him before they find anything else, because he will decide they are the leva and try to run.*

> *En la cocina hay ceniza tibia. Hay un jergón en el rincón, hay una olla, y hay un tipo agachado atrás de la puerta con una pata de mesa en la mano y la cara de un hombre que decidió hace rato que no lo agarran vivo.*

Thirty-six, red-bearded, filthy, **hiding from the press-gang**. He has no *papeleta de conchabo* — no work-pass — which in 1827 makes him legally a *vago y mal entretenido*, which means any patrol that finds him puts him in a regiment on the Brazilian frontier and that is the end of Santiago Barreto.

He has been sleeping in the kitchen of the most haunted building in the district for **five weeks**, because he correctly worked out that it is the one place the patrols will not search.

**Key Hook**: he is not brave, he is *out of options*, and he has become the world expert on this house by being too broke to leave it. He knows things nobody else knows and will trade them for one thing only: **not being handed over.**

### EL SÓTANO — EL BAÚL DE MARIANO

> *El sótano es de ladrillo, con bóveda, y está seco, lo que después de cuarenta y tres años es casi una ofensa. Contra la pared del fondo, tapado con una lona podrida que se deshace cuando la levantan, hay un baúl de cuero con herrajes.*

**Adentro:**

**1 — Seis hábitos negros.** Cheap wool, hand-sewn, sized for young men. One is much smaller than the others. **SAN 0/1** for whoever works out which of the six was seventeen.

**2 — EL POLVO PARDO** — a stoppered jar of coarse brownish powder. **Chemistry or Science (Regular)**: sulphur and copper oxide, ground fine. It is the pentagram's medium — the lines are drawn *in it*. There is **exactly enough**, and no more, which the players should be told before they start drawing.

**3 — EL POLVO BLANCO — *el polvo de Ibn-Ghazi*** — a small lead flask of fine white powder that is very slightly too heavy for its size.

> **Efecto**: thrown at the Acechador (a thrown attack, **DEX×5** or Throw), the powder makes it **visible and semi-solid for 1D6+2 rounds.** While it is powdered: **weapons and blows do full damage instead of half**, and everyone can see exactly what they are fighting, which costs **SAN 1/1D8** the first time and is worth it.
>
> **Hay tres tiradas. Tres.** Say that number out loud to the players. For two investigators these three throws are the difference between a fight and a funeral, and the tension of *when to spend the second one* is the best decision in the scenario.

**4 — LOS PAPELES DE MARIANO ALÉN** — **HANDOUT 2**. Latin extracts copied from *De Vermis Mysteriis*, heavily annotated, with the banishment worked out in Alén's own hand and — on the last two sheets, in a hastier script — **the whole chant transliterated into Spanish spelling**, for the brothers who had no Latin.

Reading and understanding them: **Occult (Regular)** or **Language (Latin)**, one hour. **SAN 1D2, Cthulhu Mythos +2%.** Either way, **tell them plainly that the working is complete and correctly worked out** — Alén's hand, cross-checked against itself twice, no gaps. This is the confirmation that used to require a library trip; it now comes free with the trunk, so a two-player table walks into midnight already knowing the ritual works.

**Si nadie tiene ninguna de las dos:** the transliteration page has Alén's own Spanish note explaining what to do, because he wrote it for people exactly this unlettered. **No roll. They can always do the ritual, and they can still be told it's complete.** Never gate the finale — or the confidence to attempt it — behind a skill on a two-person sheet.

**EL SIGNO** — a simple mark for doors and windows: a circle with five short strokes crossing its rim. ## LOS PREPARATIVOS — *la hora antes de medianoche*

*Give the players a full, unhurried scene to prepare. For two investigators this is not filler — it is where they win or lose.*

Checklist to put in front of them, out of character if necessary:

- [ ] **Trazar la estrella** — the pentagram, one continuous line. **Art/Craft or Occult (Regular).** Failure means it is subtly wrong and must be redone; a second failure means it works but the ritual runs **10 minutes longer.**
- [ ] **El arcón en el centro, abierto, boca arriba** — and getting it there needed a cart, which they should have arranged in Acto II.
- [ ] **Marcar puertas y ventanas** — **DEX roll** to distribute the powder efficiently. Count the openings out loud: the big room has **one door and three windows.** There is powder for all four **and nothing spare**; a failed DEX roll means they can only do three, and the players choose which opening to leave naked.
- [ ] **Repartir los puestos** — who chants first, who watches which window, where the polvo de Ibn-Ghazi lives and who carries it.
- [ ] **Los caballos** — anyone who leaves the horses outside loses the horses. Anyone who thinks to bring them into the *galería* or a back room keeps them, and keeps a way home.
- [ ] **Leña y luz** — the room needs light for two hours and nobody can leave to get more.

**Última oportunidad de reclutar**: Ña Petrona arrives on her own if not asked. Padre Timoteo does **not** — if he wasn't recruited, he is at El Rincón in his chapel, awake, not praying, and that is where he stays.

## ACTO IV — LA NOCHE

*Ninety minutes of table time. The scenario's reason for existing.*

> *A las doce menos cinco el viento para. No baja: para, de golpe, como cuando alguien cierra una puerta en otro cuarto. Las velas se quedan derechas. Y arriba del cielorraso, por primera vez en toda la tarde, algo se mueve — despacio, sin apuro, cruzando el alto de punta a punta.*

### EL RELOJ DEL ASEDIO

**Two columns. Use the one that matches how many warm bodies are actually in the room.** Times are ritual-clock times from midnight; if the party has enough chanters that the ritual ends before a listed beat, that beat simply never happens — **that is the reward for recruiting, and it should be visible.**

| Hora | **2 PJs solos** | **3+ cantores** |
|---|---|---|
| **00:20** | Sounds overhead; things fall off shelves; the ceiling bows. **SAN 0/1D3** | Same |
| **00:30** | Black pitch drips through the cielorraso onto the pentagram — **1D2 acid damage** to anyone under it, and **it degrades the powder lines.** Someone must repair the star (a round's work) or the ritual runs 10 min longer | Same, plus it drips on **two** points |
| **00:45** | **LA MALENA** at the window, asking to be let in | Same |
| **01:00** | *(nothing — deliberate gap; let them breathe)* | **EL COLORADO** (if dead) rises. **El toro** hits the horses |
| **01:15** | **El toro** in the *galería*, taking the door apart | Vizcachas down the chimney (nuisance, 1D2 of them) |
| **01:30** | **El toro** comes through the wall | **El toro** through the wall **+ la Malena forces a smudged window** |
| **01:55** | **EL ACECHADOR** | **EL ACECHADOR** |
| **02:00** | **Fin del ritual** *(2 chanters)* | — |

**Regla de oro del asedio**: the attackers' job is to **stop the chanting**, not to kill investigators. They go for the chanter, they go for the sigils, they go for the lights. Play them that way and the fight becomes a defence of a fixed point, which is a shape two players can actually hold.

**⏱ EL ÚLTIMO TRAMO — no lo corras en tiempo real.** The gap between the Acechador's arrival and the end of the chant is five minutes of *fictional* time, which at twelve seconds a round would be twenty-five combat rounds. **Do not run it that way** — twenty-five rounds of 70% claws at 1D8+1D6 will kill two investigators no matter how well they play, and no amount of powder covers it.

**Once the Acechador comes down, the chant needs `4 + 1D6` more rounds to land.** Roll it privately when it drops through the ceiling and count down. Five to ten rounds: long enough to be genuinely frightening, short enough that three doses of powder and a good plan are a real answer. The clock times in the table above are **pacing labels, not a combat timer** — the moment the thing is in the room, the only number that matters is this one.

*(This applies at every party size. With enough chanters the ritual simply ends before 01:55 and the Acechador never arrives at all — that remains the reward for recruiting, and it should stay visible.)*

### 01:55 — EL ACECHADOR DEL ALTILLO

#### EL ACECHADOR — BLOQUE

| STR | CON | SIZ | DEX | INT | POW | HP |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 90 | 85 | 90 | 60 | 90 | 17 |

**Move** 12 · **Ataques** 1 por asalto

| Ataque | % | Daño |
|---|---|---|
| **Garras** | 70% | **1D8 + 1D6** |
| **Escupida ácida** | 40% | 1D2, and **it dissolves a sigil at range** — this is its real use |

**DEFENSAS — the numbers that decide the fight:**
- **Invisible.** Attacks against it are at **penalty die** and it is **impossible to target reliably** without the powder. It leaves footprints in the pentagram dust; a clever player can track it that way for a normal (not penalty) attack — **let that work.**
- **Weapons and blows do HALF damage.**
- **Fire, lightning, and acid do NOTHING AT ALL.** Somebody will try burning it. Let them try, and let it be free of charge exactly once.
- **Passes through walls and ceilings freely** — **except** through an opening bearing an intact sigil. The house is not a barrier to it. The signs are.
- **Animates the dead**: 5 MP for something large, 1 MP for something small. It has been doing this all night; it starts the confrontation with roughly 12 MP left.

**⭐ EL POLVO DE IBN-GHAZI** — a hit with a throw makes it **visible and semi-solid for 1D6+2 rounds**: no penalty die, **full damage from weapons**, and it can be grappled, tripped, and pushed. **Three throws.** Two investigators who spend them well can absolutely put it down; two who spend them badly cannot. This is the scenario's central decision and it belongs entirely to the players.

**Its priority, always**: **the chanter.** It goes for whoever is speaking, every round, without cleverness. It is not tactical. It is forty-four years of being spoken at by a boy of seventeen, and now somebody is speaking at it again.

**⚠ It cannot be killed to win.** Reducing it to 0 HP **disperses** it for 1D6 rounds and then it re-forms, in the loft, whole. **The only ending is the chant reaching its end.** Make sure the players understand this before midnight — via Mariano's papers, or Rufino, or Orzábal. A two-player table that spends its finale trying to kill an unkillable thing because nobody told them is a table you failed.

**⏱ Cuánto hay que aguantar: `4 + 1D6` asaltos.** Roll it when it comes through the ceiling; that is how many rounds until the chant lands. See [El último tramo](#el-reloj-del-asedio). Sanity-check the resources against that number before you start:

| | |
|---|---|
| Rounds to survive | **5–10** (avg 7.5) |
| Powder coverage | 3 doses × 1D6+2 rounds ≈ **16 rounds** if every throw hits |
| Meaning | Two investigators who land **two of three throws** are covered for the whole fight. The third dose is their margin for a missed throw or a bad initiative — not a spare. |

**Dispersing it is still worth doing** even though it re-forms: 1D6 rounds of it being gone is 1D6 rounds the chanter is untouched, and against a 5–10 round clock that can simply *be* the win. Let a player who works that out feel clever about it.

### EL FINAL DEL CANTO

> *El que está cantando llega al final del verso — el mismo verso, la número quién sabe cuántas — y esta vez, cuando dice **descende**, la palabra no se apaga. Queda. Queda colgada en el aire del cuarto como una nota de campana.*
>
> *Y la cosa que estaba parada arriba de la estrella se dobla. No cae: se dobla, hacia el centro, hacia la boca del arcón, como el agua cuando se le saca el tapón a una batea. Y el polvo blanco que le quedaba encima se va con ella, en espiral, hacia abajo, y lo último que se lleva es el frío.*
>
> *Y el arcón se cierra solo. La tapa que hicieron falta dos hombres y una palanca para correr, se cierra sola, de golpe, y el ruido que hace es el ruido más lindo que van a escuchar en su vida.*

**Todo lo animado cae** en el mismo instante — la Malena, el toro, el Colorado si cayó — **como se cae un traje cuando lo sueltan.**

## EL DESENLACE

*Two axes. Don't editorialise about which was right.*

### ¿Se completó el canto?

**Sí** — the Acechador is in the sarcophagus, and now the sarcophagus is a problem. Mariano's papers say nothing about what to do with it, because Mariano never got this far. The players decide, and the decision should be theirs alone:
- **Al río.** Weight it, cart it to the Riachuelo or the Salado, sink it. Out of reach; not destroyed.
- **Enterrado.** Under the casco, deep, and the deed is in their names, so nobody will ever build there. The tidiest answer and the one that makes them Rufino.
- **A la Universidad.** Orzábal would take it. Orzábal should not have it. If they do this, end the scenario on Orzábal running an approving hand along the lid.
- **Abrirlo para ver.** Somebody always suggests it. Do not stop them.

**No** — the chant broke, or nobody was left to speak. The thing is loose at dawn, and it is not bound to anything any more. El Rincón has perhaps a month. The investigators can try again at the next midnight if they survived the night and can still stand — **and the sarcophagus and the powder are still there** — but they will be doing it with whoever is left.

### ¿Y don Rufino?

He dies on the fourth night regardless of anything the investigators do. **When** matters:
- **If the ritual completed first**, doña Ágata reports that he went easily, and that he asked her a question she did not understand and then laughed: *"¿Se acabó? ¿De verdad se acabó?"*
- **If it did not**, he died at some point during the night, and everyone in that room knows roughly when, because they were there when it got stronger.

### La parte legal — *no se saltea*

There are bodies. There is a demolished wall. There is a dead woman from El Rincón who has been missing three weeks and is now unmistakably, recently dead in a house **the investigators legally own**, having been seen riding out there by the whole village.

The **juez de paz** comes. **Bernabé Miraval**, who wanted that deed back, is entirely willing to say in writing that two men from the campaña talked his dying father into signing over property.

- **Ña Petrona alive** = they are fine. Her word is worth more in that district than any paper, and she gives it without being asked.
- **El padre Timoteo alive** = they are fine, and it costs him something to say it, and he says it anyway.
- **Neither ally survived** = a genuinely nasty epilogue. Play it out in five minutes. Two frontier men, no witnesses, a dead woman and a burned-out house. **Modo pulp**: they get out of it — but they get out of it *running*, and they do not get to go back to that district for a long time.

## RECOMPENSAS

### 1 — Cordura
- **+1D10 SAN** if the banishment completed.
- **+1D6** if they survived the night without completing it but the thing is still contained by circumstance (they broke off before midnight and never started, and Rufino is still alive).
- **+1D4 adicional** if **every recruited ally lived.** Announce this one at the table; it is the reward the scenario is actually about.
- **Ninguna** if the chant broke and the thing is loose.

### 2 — Chequeos de Experiencia
Standard 7e — tick every skill successfully used, roll each against its current value, failure grants **+1D10%**. Likely tonight: Persuade, Psychology, Occult, Library Use, Spot Hidden, Track, Natural World, Fighting, Firearms, Dodge, First Aid, Throw, Art/Craft. With two players this is a meaningful chunk of advancement — do not skip it.

### 3 — El campo
They own an *estancia*. It is a ruined casco, four leagues of decent grazing, and a legal title signed by a dying man whose son disputes it. Worth something eventually; worth trouble immediately. **Write it on the sheet.**

### 4 — Objetos y vínculos con nombre

**El papel del Colorado** — *Unlock*: he lives, and they sign it.
Not an item. A *papeleta de conchabo* in an investigator's own hand makes Santiago Barreto legally a person again, and he knows exactly who did that. **A permanent contact**: a man who can ride anything, track anything, hide anywhere in the southern campaña, and who owes them the thing that cannot be repaid. He will not take money from them ever again and will be annoyed if they offer.

**El polvo que sobró** — *Unlock*: they didn't spend all three throws.
Any remaining doses of **polvo de Ibn-Ghazi**, in their lead flask. One dose, one throw, **1D6+2 rounds of a thing being visible and solid.** Do not let them make more; nobody alive knows how. This is a bullet with one use and no replacement, and a player will carry it for three campaigns waiting for the right moment.

**El rosario de Ña Petrona** — *Unlock*: she chanted, she lived, and somebody thanked her properly.
Worn olive-wood beads, sixty years of one woman's hands. **No mechanical effect whatsoever.** She presses it on whichever investigator looked worst at dawn, refuses to discuss it, and goes to open the pulpería because people will want breakfast.

**Los papeles de Mariano Alén** — *Unlock*: taken rather than burned.
The full transliterated banishment, verified. Genuinely useful and genuinely dangerous, and any Keeper running a longer campaign now has a party who know a working. **+2% Cthulhu Mythos** on a full study. Alén's marginalia also mention, twice, *"los otros que también preguntaban"* — the others who were also asking. Never explained. Leave it.
