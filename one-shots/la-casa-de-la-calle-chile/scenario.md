# LA CASA DE LA CALLE CHILE

*One-shot — Call of Cthulhu 7e, modo Pulp · Buenos Aires, 1822 · 3–4 horas · 3–5 investigadores*

*A port of "The Haunting" (the classic Corbitt House scenario) to Rivadavian Buenos Aires. Same-world, light tie-in to La Tierra Mala: same city, same register and voseo (see `assets/spanish-style-guide.md`), **no plot connection** — the house is in Monserrat, deliberately far from the Manzana de las Luces and the vault. Nothing here needs the campaign to make sense, and nothing here contradicts it.*

---

## CÓMO USAR ESTE DOCUMENTO

- **Read-aloud** blocks are in Spanish, blockquoted, period-register (see the style guide). Read them straight off the page.
- **Keeper notes** are in English, plain text, under each block.
- **Modo Pulp, house rules for tonight** (same house rules as `../lo-que-se-agarra-del-subterraneo/scenario.md`, so the two one-shots feel like the same series):
  - **HP ×2** on all investigator sheets, for this session only.
  - **Bennies**: each investigator may, once per scene, auto-succeed a failed roll by narrating how — *"el farol se me cayó pero quedó prendido,"* *"tenía el facón en la bota, no en la cintura."* Keeper's call on whether the justification is good enough; if it is, let it ride.
  - Play it like a serial matinee. Corvalán is a **physical, chaseable, killable thing** — a dead man who can be shot, burned, buried, and broken — not an inevitability. Let them do the stupid heroic thing and have it *work*, sometimes.
  - **Pulp talents** (optional, one per investigator, pick at the table): *Duro de matar* (regain 1D6 HP once per session, free action) · *Puntería* (one Firearms roll per session is an automatic Hard success) · *Nervios de acero* (ignore one SAN loss per session, entirely) · *Manos rápidas* (draw and act first in any round you'd otherwise be surprised).
- **Duration math** (see the [Runsheet](#runsheet--34-horas)): Contrato ~15 min · Investigación en la ciudad ~80 min · La casa ~70 min · Sótano y final ~30 min · Cierre ~15 min. The city block is the one to cut if you're running long.
- **Language note**: every read-aloud is Spanish. Keeper mechanics, stat blocks, and structural guidance are English. If a block has an English line under it in italics with an em-dash, that's a gloss for you — **don't read it out**.

## LA VERDAD DEL KEEPER

*(Not for players. Read once before the table opens.)*

**Don Ruperto Corvalán** came over from Cádiz around 1755 and made his money the way money was made here: hides, tallow, salt. He built the house on **calle Chile** in **1779** and lived in it alone for twenty-nine years with no wife, no children, and a great deal of correspondence.

He was also the patron and, quietly, the head of the **Hermandad de Nuestro Señor de los Secretos** — a lay brotherhood that held its offices in a small chapel south of the city, the **Capilla de la Contemplación**. In 1794 his neighbours brought a complaint before the **Cabildo**: *costumbres impías, trato con gente de mal vivir, ruidos a deshora.* The suit went nowhere. Corvalán had money and the Cabildo had wars to worry about.

He died in **1808**. His will instructed that he be buried **in the sótano of his own house**, not in consecrated ground. The neighbours petitioned to stop it. The petition was still moving through the Cabildo's paperwork when the English came back, and then the Revolution came, and nobody ever got around to it. **He is still down there.** He was never entirely finished with being alive, and the years since have made him less finished, not more.

The chapel outlived him by nine years. In **1817** an *alcalde de barrio* raided it on a rumour that the brotherhood was taking children out of the southern barrios. The raid was a slaughter — **three of the alcalde's men and seventeen of the brotherhood died** in a building with no windows. Fifty-four were arrested; eight were convicted. **Fray Miguel Tomás**, the defrocked Franciscan who presided, drew forty years. He **walked out of the cárcel during the Anarquía de 1820**, when the province had three governors in a single day and nobody was minding the doors. He has not been seen since. He is not in this scenario — but somebody has been **repainting the symbols on the chapel wall in fresh white lime**, and the players should notice that and be unable to do anything about it.

Since 1808 the house has eaten three families:
- **1810** — a family flees inside a year. Accidents, then two deaths. Nobody investigates; it is 1810 and the city has other things on its mind.
- **1812–1817** — a second family. A wasting sickness that no médico could name; the eldest son hangs himself in the *cuarto de huéspedes*.
- **1821–1822** — the **familia Macario**, Genoese, six weeks in the house. **Vitorio Macario** is now in the men's ward of the Hospital General; **Gabriela Macario** is in the Convalecencia. Their children were sent to relatives in Montevideo.

**Corvalán's hatred is specific**: he hates whichever man holds the house. Women and children he torments; the master of the house he tries to destroy. This matters for the endings.

**Tonight's engine**: Rivadavia's ecclesiastical reform (February 1822) has dumped a great deal of dissolved-order and disputed property onto the market at once, and **Don Anselmo Braña**, *rematador*, bought this one cheap without asking why it was cheap. He cannot sell it, cannot rent it, and cannot get a priest to bless it — the Church has other problems this year. So he is hiring gauchos, because gauchos are cheap, armed, and not from here.

## LOS INVESTIGADORES

Written for **3–5 investigators**. The default framing, and the one that plays best, is the campaign's: **frontier men in a port city that no longer knows what to do with them** — armed, competent, conspicuous, and treated by every clerk and porter in this document as slightly less than a person. Lean on that. It is free tension and it is historically exact.

Alternatives if the table wants something else: a *vigilante* of the new Jefatura and his friends, doing this off the books; a Genoese sailor waiting out a ship; a *médico* from the Hospital who treated Vitorio Macario and cannot let it go. All work. Only the gaucho framing gets the free hostility.

## EL CONTRATO — DON ANSELMO BRAÑA

*Open here. Fifteen minutes, no longer.*

A back room off a *rematería* on calle Perú, smelling of paper and cold cigar. Braña is fifty, soft-handed, well-dressed in a coat two seasons out of Paris fashion, and sweating in a way the weather does not explain.

> *—Se los voy a decir derecho, porque me parece que ustedes son gente de decir las cosas derecho. Compré una casa en la calle Chile, en Monserrat, buena casa, de altos, con dos patios y aljibe. La compré barata. La compré barata porque en este barrio dicen que está embrujada.*
>
> *Se ríe. La risa no le llega a ningún lado.*
>
> *—Yo no creo en eso. Que quede dicho: yo no creo en eso. Pero no me la alquila nadie, no me la compra nadie, y el cura de San Juan me dijo que este año la Iglesia tiene otras cosas que bendecir. Así que necesito un papel. Necesito que alguien entre, pase la noche, y me firme que adentro no hay nada más que humedad y ratas.*

**The offer**: **cuatro pesos fuertes por día, por cabeza** — a working man's month in a week — plus **veinte pesos fuertes** on delivery of a signed statement that the house is sound. He will pay two days in advance, in silver, out of a strongbox, if pressed. He wants it done **this week**.

**Braña's answers**, if asked:

**¿Qué pasó ahí?**
> *—Una familia de genoveses, los Macario. Estuvieron seis semanas. Él terminó en el Hospital de Hombres y ella en la Convalecencia, y los chicos se los llevó un tío a Montevideo. Eso es lo que sé y es todo lo que sé, y si supiera más se los diría, porque a mí lo que me interesa es venderla, no defenderla.*

**¿Es verdad que hay alguien enterrado adentro?**
> *(Se pone muy quieto.)* *—Eso me lo dijeron. También me dijeron que el dueño viejo era brujo, y que había una cofradía, y que la policía la cerró a los tiros hace unos años. En esta ciudad le dicen brujo a cualquiera que cierre las persianas de día. Yo compré una casa, no una historia.*

**¿Por qué nosotros?**
> *—Porque los porteños de acá se persignan y me dicen que no. Y porque ustedes, discúlpenme la franqueza, no le tienen miedo a lo mismo que le tiene miedo la gente de la ciudad.*

**Keeper**: Braña is not a villain and is not hiding anything sinister — he is a greedy man who bought a problem. He is, however, **the man who holds the house**, which makes him the person Corvalán most wants dead. Keep him alive and available; he becomes the pivot of the bad ending.

He hands over **the key** — a heavy old thing — and warns them, offhand, that the front door has *"como cinco cerraduras, todas por adentro, no me pregunten por qué."*

**Braña, quick block** (he does not fight): STR 45 CON 50 SIZ 65 DEX 40 INT 70 POW 45 EDU 65 APP 55 · HP 11 · Persuade 65%, Accounting 60%, Bargain 70%, Law 40%.

## LA CIUDAD — LA INVESTIGACIÓN

*Six locations. **Nobody needs all six.** Three is a complete picture; four is generous. Let the players choose their own order and cut whatever they don't reach — every critical fact appears in at least two places, listed under [Redundancia de pistas](#redundancia-de-pistas) at the end of this section.*

**Pacing rule for the whole block**: no location should take more than fifteen minutes. If a scene is stalling, have the NPC volunteer the clue and move on. The city is the appetiser; the house is the meal.

### LA IMPRENTA DE LOS NIÑOS EXPÓSITOS

*The foundling-house press on calle Perú — the press that has printed nearly everything in this city since 1780. Bandeja de tipos, ink, the smell of hot lead and wet paper.*

> *El taller es un cuarto largo, caluroso, con una prensa de madera en el medio y cuatro hombres trabajando sin levantar la vista. Contra la pared del fondo, hasta el techo, atados con piolín: los años. Gacetas viejas, bandos, esquelas, todo amarillo, todo junto, todo sin orden que nadie de afuera pueda entender.*

**Access**: the front of the shop will not let strangers into the back. Getting past requires **Persuade, Fast Talk, Charm o Intimidate (Regular)** — or simply money, which works fine and is very period; two reales to the boy at the door.

#### ARTEMIO QUIROGA — *el cajista*

Thin, red-haired, spectacles thick enough to distort his eyes, teeth stained brown from *mate* and tobacco, ink in every crease of both hands. He has worked here nineteen years and he is the only person alive who can find anything in that wall of paper.

**Key Hook**: Artemio is not obstructive out of malice — he is **proud**, and nobody has asked his professional opinion about anything in nineteen years. Treat him as an expert and he opens the wall. Treat him as a doorman and he becomes one.

**Si lo tratan bien:** he spends an hour in the paper with them and finds two things.

#### LO QUE ENCUENTRA ARTEMIO

**1 — Lo que no se imprimió.** A set article, composed in lead and then killed before it ran. Artemio still has the galley proof because he never breaks up a form he's proud of.

> *—Esta la armé yo en el veintiuno y nunca salió. Me la bajaron de arriba. Dijeron que no convenía. Yo la guardé porque el trabajo era bueno.*

**HANDOUT 1 — La galera que no salió** *(read aloud or hand over — see [Handouts](#handouts))*

> *LA CASA DE LA CALLE CHILE. Vecinos del barrio de Monserrat solicitan a esta redacción noticia de la finca sita en la calle Chile, que en el espacio de doce años ha visto salir de sus puertas tres familias y entrar en ellas tres desgracias. En el año diez la abandonó una familia de comerciantes tras dos muertes que el vecindario atribuyó a accidente. Entre el doce y el diecisiete la habitó otra, que padeció una dolencia que ningún facultativo supo nombrar, y de la cual el hijo mayor se dio muerte por su propia mano en el cuarto de huéspedes. Consta que la finca fue levantada en 1779 por don Ruperto Corvalán, comerciante en cueros, cuyo entierro dentro de la propia casa fue motivo de representación ante el Ilustre Cabildo en el año ocho.*

**2 — La quema.** If asked why there's so little from before 1806:

> *—Lo viejo viejo no está. Se perdió con los ingleses, en el seis y el siete. Lo que no se quemó se repartió, y lo que se repartió no volvió. De antes de esa fecha, acá, hay poco y malo. Si quieren cosas viejas, vayan a la Biblioteca o al Cabildo, y apúrense con el Cabildo, que lo están desarmando.*

**Keeper**: that last line is a **pointer to the Archivo**, and a real deadline — the Cabildo was abolished at the end of 1821 and its papers are being crated. Use it.

### LA BIBLIOTECA PÚBLICA

*In the Manzana de las Luces, founded 1810, twelve years old and already too small. Long tables, poor light, a librarian who takes the investigators' presence as a personal affront.*

> *Adentro huele a papel húmedo y a cera. Hay tres mesas largas, dos estudiantes discutiendo en latín en voz demasiado alta, y un hombre detrás de un mostrador que los mira entrar y decide, en el acto y para siempre, lo que piensa de ustedes.*

**Access**: the librarian will not seat armed frontier men without a reason. **Persuade (Regular)**, a letter from Braña (he'll write one if asked — he wants this done), or **Credit Rating** if anyone has it. **Intimidate works and costs**: they get in, and a boy is sent to fetch a *vigilante*, who arrives twenty minutes later and needs handling.

**Library Use (Regular)** — a solid hour's work, two successes' worth if the table is enjoying it:

- **La construcción.** Property records: the house was built in **1779** for **don Ruperto Corvalán**, *comerciante en cueros*, on a lot he bought outright. No mortgage, no partner. He paid in silver.
- **El pleito del noventa y cuatro.** A summary of a suit brought before the Cabildo by four neighbours in **1794**, alleging *"costumbres impías, trato con gente de mal vivir, y ruidos y luces a deshora en la finca de la calle Chile."* The suit was dismissed for lack of evidence. **One of the four complainants died before it was heard.**
- **La muerte.** Corvalán died in **1808**, aged approximately seventy-five. Cause of death recorded as *"natural."* The record of burial is **not in the parish books**, which a **Hard success** or a pointed question makes explicit: *he is not buried in any churchyard in this city.*

**History (Regular)**, layered on top, gets the context: the Hermandad de Nuestro Señor de los Secretos is mentioned twice in passing in the Biblioteca's holdings as *"una cofradía de legos del barrio del sur"* — a lay brotherhood, unremarkable in a city with dozens of them, except that neither mention names a single member other than Corvalán.

### EL ARCHIVO DEL CABILDO

*The best scene in the city block. The Cabildo has been abolished — its functions dissolved at the end of last year — and three centuries of municipal paper is sitting on the floor in crates, half-labelled, being fought over by clerks who no longer know who they work for.*

> *El salón está vacío de muebles y lleno de cajones. Tres siglos de papel, apilados sin orden, con las tapas abiertas y los legajos asomando. Un escribiente viejo camina entre las pilas con una lista en la mano, tachando cosas, y cuando ustedes entran levanta la vista con una cara que dice, más claro que cualquier palabra: yo ya no soy nadie acá, hagan lo que quieran.*

**Access**: essentially free. Nobody is guarding this. That is the point, and it should feel slightly obscene — the city's memory is on the floor and anyone can walk in.

**Spot Hidden or Library Use (Regular)**, plus a good half hour of digging:

- **El testamento de Corvalán (1808).** He left everything to the Hermandad. His executor — *albacea* — is named: **fray Miguel Tomás**.
- **La representación de los vecinos (1808).** The neighbours' petition to prevent the burial. It is unresolved. There is a note in a clerk's hand on the back, dated 1809: *"pendiente."* It has been pending for fourteen years.

**HANDOUT 2 — Del testamento de don Ruperto Corvalán, 1808**

> *Ítem: que mi cuerpo no sea llevado a iglesia ni a camposanto, sino sepultado en el sótano de mi propia casa de la calle Chile, en el nicho que dejo dispuesto y pagado, y que se tape la boca del nicho con mampostería y no con losa ni con nombre. Ítem: que se cierren las ventanas de la casa con clavo y no con llave. Esto se ha de cumplir conforme a mi voluntad y al deseo de aquel que aguarda en lo oscuro, y de ninguna otra manera.*
>
> *— "…nailed shut, not locked… in accordance with my will and with the desire of the one who waits in the dark."*

**Keeper**: *"el deseo de aquel que aguarda en lo oscuro"* is the single most important line in the city block. Let it sit. Don't explain it — nothing in this scenario ever explains it, and the players should leave the table still wondering what Corvalán was waiting *for*.

**La Cofradía (1817).** In the same crates, if they keep digging: the licence of the Hermandad de Nuestro Señor de los Secretos, revoked in **1817**, with the reason given as *"por auto de la justicia,"* and a cross-reference to a case file that is **not here** — it went to the new Jefatura de Policía when the Cabildo's policing role was transferred. **This is the pointer to the next location.**

### LA JEFATURA DE POLICÍA

*A year-old institution in a building it doesn't fit, staffed by men who are not sure what their own authority is. They are earnest, understaffed, and deferential to anyone who sounds important.*

> *La Jefatura funciona en una casa que no fue hecha para esto. Hay escritorios en lo que era un comedor y legajos en lo que era una despensa. Los hombres son jóvenes, tienen uniformes nuevos que todavía no les quedan bien, y miran a ustedes — armados, de campo, con las botas embarradas — con una mezcla exacta de sospecha y de ganas de que alguien les diga qué hacer.*

**Access**: **Persuade, Credit Rating, or Law (Regular)**. Braña's letter helps enormously. **Intimidate is a bad idea here** and should have consequences — these men are nervous and armed and they will remember faces.

**El expediente de 1817** — the raid on the Capilla de la Contemplación:

- The *alcalde de barrio* took nineteen men into the chapel on the night of **14 August 1817**, acting on complaints that the brotherhood was taking children out of the southern barrios.
- **Three of his men died. Seventeen of the brotherhood died.** In a building with no windows, in the dark.
- **Fifty-four arrests. Eight convictions.** The rest were released for lack of evidence, or because the evidence was in the building and the building was, by then, mostly on fire.
- **Fray Miguel Tomás** — sentenced to forty years. **Escaped in 1820.** The file notes, in a very small hand, that the escape occurred *"en los días de la anarquía"* and that no pursuit was mounted.

**El detalle que importa (Spot Hidden or Medicine, Regular)**: the autopsies of the seventeen dead cultists are **wrong**. Not falsified — *vague*. Cause of death is recorded, for eleven of the seventeen, as *"heridas."* Wounds. No weapon named, no location on the body, nothing. A **Medicine success** or any character with battlefield experience knows that a surgeon writes *"heridas"* with no further detail for exactly one reason: he does not want to write down what he saw.

**SAN 0/1** for reading the file carefully.

> *—Ese expediente lo heredamos nosotros del Cabildo, señor, y con todo respeto: ojalá no lo hubiéramos heredado. Los que estuvieron esa noche no hablan. Yo pregunté una vez. Una sola vez.*

### EL BARRIO — MONSERRAT

*The neighbourhood itself, half a block from the Tambor. Run this as a walking scene, not a location. Drumming somewhere south on feast evenings; the smell of the saladeros when the wind turns; children in the street until dark and then not.*

The house is known. Everyone on the block knows it. Nobody wants to be the one who says so.

#### ÑO DOROTEO BRACAMONTE — *el aguatero*

Sixty-odd, Afro-Argentine, free since before it was common, and the single most useful person in this document. He has driven the water-cart in this barrio for thirty years, which means **he has been inside every patio on this street**, filling every aljibe, twice a week, for three decades.

**Approach**: **APP or Credit Rating (Regular)** for the direct approach; but the real answer is that Doroteo talks to anyone who helps him with the cart, buys him a gin, or simply treats him as a man with a trade rather than a source. **No roll needed** for a player who does any of those. Multiple attempts allowed with different skills — this is a deliberately un-gateable NPC.

**Key Hook**: he is not frightened and he is not superstitious. He is **precise**. He tells you what he saw and declines to tell you what it means, because he considers that somebody else's job.

**Diálogos — Ño Doroteo**

**Si preguntan por la casa:**
> *—Esa casa yo le lleno el aljibe desde antes que ustedes tuvieran dientes. Se la llené a don Ruperto, que en paz no descansa, y se la llené a las tres familias que vinieron después. Tres. Y a ninguna se la terminé de llenar el año entero.*

**Si preguntan por los Macario:**
> *—Buena gente. Genoveses. Él trabajaba en el saladero y ella cosía. Entraron en febrero, gordos y colorados los dos. En marzo él ya no me saludaba. En abril lo vi en el zaguán y les juro por lo que quieran que el hombre había perdido veinte libras y el pelo se le había puesto blanco de este lado nomás* —se toca la sien izquierda— *de este lado nomás, como si le hubieran apoyado una mano.*

**Si preguntan qué le pasó a él (esta es la pista que importa):**
> *—Se lo llevaron en mayo, atado, gritando en genovés. Y la mujer... la mujer no gritaba. La mujer salió caminando sola, con los dos chicos de la mano, y se paró en la vereda y se dio vuelta y le habló a la casa. No le habló a nadie. Le habló a la casa.*

**Si preguntan si él vio algo:**
> *(Se toma su tiempo. Sirve agua de la carreta que nadie pidió, para tener algo que hacer con las manos.)*
>
> *—Una vez. Una sola. Estaba llenando el aljibe del segundo patio, de noche, tarde, porque ese mes andaba atrasado. Y levanté la vista para la ventana de arriba, la del cuarto del fondo. Y había un hombre mirándome. Flaco. Muy alto. Y los ojos le brillaban amarillos como un tizón cuando lo soplás.*
>
> *—No me corrí. Terminé de llenar el aljibe, porque el trabajo es el trabajo. Pero desde esa noche le cobro a esa casa el doble y le lleno el aljibe de día.*

**SAN 0/1** on hearing this told plainly by a man who is obviously not lying.

**Si preguntan por el barrio / la cofradía:**
> *—De la cofradía no le pregunte a mí, pregúntele a los viejos del Tambor, y no le van a contestar tampoco. En el diecisiete se llevaron gente de acá. Chicos. Nunca aparecieron y nunca hubo quien respondiera. Este barrio tiene memoria y la memoria de este barrio no es para vender.*

**Keeper**: do not turn this into a second investigation thread — it is closure and weight, not a lead. If the players push for names, the answer is silence, and the silence is the point.

### LOS HOSPITALES — LOS MACARIO

*Two scenes, two buildings, because Buenos Aires in 1822 does not put men and women in the same hospital. Run both if the table has time; run **Gabriela alone** if they don't — she has all the load-bearing information.*

#### VITORIO MACARIO — Hospital General de Hombres, sala de dementes

> *La sala es larga y tiene doce camas y huele a lo que huelen estas salas. Vitorio Macario está sentado en la suya, con las piernas cruzadas como un sastre, sosteniendo una Biblia contra el pecho con las dos manos. No la lee. La sostiene. Tiene cuarenta y un años y el pelo blanco de un solo lado.*

He does not respond to questions, to his name, to his own language, or to being touched. He speaks only in scripture, and only in fragments, and always the same category of fragment: **divine punishment**.

> *—...y la casa del impío será asolada... (una pausa larga)... y sus habitaciones desoladas... y no quedará de él ni nombre ni memoria en la tierra...*

**Psychology or Medicine (Regular)**: he is not catatonic. He is **hiding**. There is a functioning man in there who has made a considered decision not to come out.

**Si le mencionan la calle Chile, o la casa, o el nombre Corvalán** — one beat, one time only, and then he is gone again:

> *Deja de recitar. Levanta la cabeza. Los mira a los ojos por primera vez, y por un segundo hay un hombre entero ahí adentro, y ese hombre está aterrado y perfectamente cuerdo.*
>
> *—No es la casa. Es el que está abajo de la casa. Y no me quería a mí. Quería que yo estuviera ahí para poder odiarme.*
>
> *Y vuelve a la Biblia, y no vuelve a salir esta noche ni ninguna otra.*

**SAN 0/1D3.**

#### GABRIELA MACARIO — Hospital de Mujeres, la Convalecencia

*The important interview. Gabriela is entirely lucid, and that is what makes her frightening.*

> *La sientan con ustedes en un patio con una parra y dos naranjos, y por un momento uno podría creer que está en cualquier otra parte. Tiene treinta y ocho años, la cara flaca, las manos quietas sobre la falda. Habla despacio y en orden, como quien ya contó esto varias veces y aprendió que si lo cuenta rápido no le creen.*

**Key Hook**: she is not mad and she knows she is not mad, and she is here because a lucid woman describing what she described is easier to institutionalise than to believe. Play her with dignity. Any investigator who treats her as a patient gets less than one who treats her as a witness.

**Diálogos — Gabriela Macario**

**Qué pasaba en la casa:**
> *—Golpes. Primero eso nomás, golpes, y una piensa que es la madera, que la casa se asienta. Pero la madera golpea cuando cambia el tiempo, y esto golpeaba cuando estábamos todos juntos y callados. Golpeaba cuando había alguien a quien asustar. Eso no lo hace la madera.*

**Las cosas que se movían:**
> *—Se caían los cuadros. Se abrían las puertas. Un plato se me fue de la mesa y cruzó el comedor entero y se rompió contra la pared del fondo — no se cayó, señores, cruzó. Yo lo vi. Los chicos lo vieron.*

**El olor:**
> *—Y el olor. En el cuarto de huéspedes, arriba. Pescado podrido. Carne podrida. Aparecía y se iba, y no había nada, y una limpiaba igual porque qué va a hacer una.*

**Lo que le pasó a Vitorio (la pista central):**
> *—A mí me asustaba. A los chicos los asustaba. A mi marido lo estaba matando, y no es lo mismo, y yo me di cuenta tarde.*
>
> *—A mí me tiraba los platos. A él le hablaba. De noche, en la cama, yo lo escuchaba a Vitorio contestarle a alguien, y no había nadie, y le contestaba con respeto, ¿entiende? Como se le contesta a un patrón. Y a la mañana no se acordaba de nada.*
>
> *—Perdió veinte libras en dos meses. Se le puso el pelo blanco de un lado. Una noche me desperté y él no estaba en la cama y lo encontré parado arriba de la boca del sótano, descalzo, dormido, con la tranca ya sacada.*

**SAN 0/1D4** on the last image.

**Si preguntan si vio algo ella misma:**
> *—Una vez. En el agua del aljibe, del segundo patio, agachada para sacar, con el farol en la mano. Vi la cara mía y atrás de la cara mía había dos luces rojas que me miraban desde abajo del agua. Y el aljibe tiene tres varas de hondo y agua de lluvia y no tiene nada más adentro.*
>
> *—No grité. Me acuerdo perfectamente que no grité. Solté el balde y me fui para adentro caminando, sin correr, porque si corría se hacía verdad.*

**SAN 0/1D3.**

**Si le preguntan qué deberían hacer:**
> *—Yo no soy quién para decirles. Pero si van a entrar, entren de día y hagan lo que tengan que hacer abajo, en el sótano, porque es ahí. Todo lo de arriba es para que uno no baje.*
>
> *— "Everything upstairs is there so that you don't go down."*

**Keeper**: that is the thesis of the whole scenario, said out loud by the only person who survived it intact. If the party reaches Gabriela, they walk into the house knowing where to go. **That is fine.** The house still has to be crossed.

**Si la presionan demasiado / si se ponen bruscos**: she does not break down — the nuns do it for her. Two sisters appear, the interview is over, and getting a second one requires a **Hard Persuade** and coming back another day, which costs a night.

### LA CAPILLA DE LA CONTEMPLACIÓN

*South of the city, past the last regular blocks, where the grid gives up and the land goes to reeds and the Riachuelo smells of hides and lime. Optional but strongly recommended — this is where the tome is, and the tome is the scenario's one real piece of forbidden knowledge.*

> *La capilla está a media legua del último rancho, sola en un pastizal, con las puertas arrancadas y el techo abierto en dos lugares. Es chica. Nunca fue linda. Y en la pared del frente, arriba de donde estaba la puerta, hay una figura pintada con cal blanca: tres letras íes griegas puestas en triángulo, y en el medio del triángulo, un ojo.*
>
> *La cal está fresca. Alguien la repintó este año.*

**SAN 0/1** for the fresh lime, once someone works out what it means.

**Keeper**: nothing comes of this. Fray Miguel Tomás does not appear in this scenario. The point is that the players leave the table knowing that somebody is still keeping this building painted, and that they never find out who. **Resist every temptation to resolve it.**

**El sótano de la capilla**: the floor at the rear has partly collapsed into a crypt beneath. Getting down is a **Climb (Regular)**; a failure is a bruising six-foot drop, 1D3 damage, not a plot stopper.

> *Abajo hay agua hasta el tobillo y hace mucho más frío del que debería hacer. Contra las paredes, sentados, con la espalda apoyada y las piernas estiradas como quien se sentó a descansar: siete esqueletos con hábitos de seda. La seda aguantó mejor que ellos. Y en el medio, un arcón quemado, y adentro del arcón lo que quedó de los papeles de la cofradía.*

**SAN 0/1D6** for the seven seated skeletons.

**Los papeles quemados** — mostly destroyed. Two things survive:

**HANDOUT 3 — Del diario de la Hermandad**

> *Día de San Bartolomé del año de 1808. Se dio sepultura a nuestro hermano don Ruperto Corvalán en el sótano de su casa de la calle Chile, conforme a su voluntad y al deseo de aquel que aguarda en lo oscuro. Tapióse la boca del nicho con mampostería, sin losa y sin nombre, y se clavaron las ventanas de la casa desde adentro, según él lo dejó dispuesto y pagado. No lloramos, porque él nos tenía prohibido llorar, y porque no se llora por quien no se ha ido.*
>
> *— "…we did not weep, because he had forbidden us to weep, and because one does not weep for a man who has not gone."*

**EL LIBER IVONIS** — a hand-copied Latin manuscript, water-damaged and burned along one edge, roughly a third legible.

- **Reading it**: 8 days of study (or a session-appropriate abstraction — in a one-shot, let a player who commits an hour on-site skim it for the surface layer).
- **Cthulhu Mythos +2%** for the legible portion. **SAN 1D3.**
- **Language (Latin)** required, or a translator. If nobody at the table has Latin: the manuscript is a *hand copy*, and the copyist wrote marginal glosses in Spanish, which gets a Latin-less party the gist at half value (**+1% Mythos**, same SAN).
- **The useful part**: it names the practice of *sepultura viva* — the deliberate burial of a living or willing adept in unhallowed ground beneath a dwelling, "so that the house may be his body." It is not an instruction manual. It is a description of a thing that people have done. **This tells the players exactly what Corvalán is, and it does not tell them how to stop him** — which is correct, because the answer to Corvalán is a shovel and a torch, not a counter-ritual.

## LA CASA DE LA CALLE CHILE

*The main event. Budget seventy minutes and be ready to spend ninety.*

### EL EXTERIOR

> *La casa es de las viejas, de las de antes del virreinato nuevo: paredes gruesas de ladrillo revocado, reja en la ventana de la calle, portón de dos hojas con clavos de bronce. A los costados, los vecinos le construyeron encima con los años, dos casas de altos que la dejaron metida en el medio como un diente flojo entre dos sanos. Nunca le da el sol.*
>
> *Las ventanas de la planta baja están tapiadas con tablas. Fíjense de qué lado están los clavos.*

**Spot Hidden (Regular)**, or simply looking: **the boards are nailed from the inside.** So are the upper windows. Every window in this house was sealed from within, and there is no way to nail a board from inside a room you then leave by the door and lock behind you — unless you were never planning to leave.

**SAN 0/1** when a player works that out themselves. **No SAN loss if the Keeper says it for them** — make them get there.

**El fondo**: a rear garden gone entirely to weed, with a collapsed *parral* (grape arbour) and a fig tree that has done far too well.

**La puerta**: the key Braña gave them works the lock. The door does not open, because there are **three bolts and a bar** on the inside as well. Options: the boarded rear window (**STR or Mechanical Repair, Regular**), the *azotea* from a neighbour's roof (**Climb, Regular**, plus talking a neighbour into it — good scene), or brute force on the front door (**STR Hard**, loud, and the whole street watches gauchos break into a house, which the Keeper should absolutely remember for the endings).

### PLANTA BAJA

*A **casa chorizo**: rooms strung along one side of two patios, front to back. Sketch it on a napkin — zaguán, first patio, second patio, and the rooms hanging off the left-hand wall.*

> *Adentro está oscuro a las tres de la tarde. Lo que hay en el aire no es olor a cerrado — es más específico que eso, y ninguno de ustedes va a decir en voz alta a qué se parece. El primer patio tiene el piso de baldosa levantado por una raíz. En una maceta rajada quedan los palitos de lo que alguien plantó y no llegó a ver crecer.*

**Light**: there is none. Everything the players see tonight, they see by something they are carrying and can drop. Say so out loud, once, early.

#### 1 — EL ZAGUÁN Y LA CANCEL
The entry passage and its inner iron-and-glass door. Hooks with three coats still on them. A pair of boots, side by side, toes to the wall. Umbrellas. A basket of *carbón*.

The side door off the zaguán is secured with **three bolts and two keyed locks, all on the inside.** Same story as the windows.

#### 2 — LA SALA
The parlour. Furniture under sheets. A *clavicordio* with four strings gone. Crucifixes — **many** crucifixes, far more than a normal devout household, on every wall, and a **Spot Hidden** notices they are of different ages and styles: each family added more.

> *Contá los crucifijos. Hay diecinueve en un cuarto donde no vivía nadie. Los tres primeros son viejos, de plata, buenos. Los otros dieciséis son cada vez más baratos y cada vez más nuevos, y el último es de dos palitos atados con hilo de coser.*

**SAN 0/1.** Three families, escalating.

#### 3 — EL COMEDOR
Oak table, built-in *aparador*, **seven chairs**. **Three places set** — plates, spoons, a serving dish — with the remains of a *puchero* in them that has been there since May.

> *Tres platos servidos. La comida se secó y se puso negra y le creció encima algo que ya se murió también. Las sillas están corridas para atrás, las tres. Nadie se levantó despacio de esta mesa.*

#### 4 — LA COCINA
Fogón, iron pots, a rat-ruined larder. Some provisions salvageable. The **water for the house comes from the aljibe in the second patio** — establish this now, because they will go there.

#### 5 — LA DESPENSA — **LOS DIARIOS**
*The single most important room on this floor.*

A locked *alacena* (cupboard) — **Locksmith (Regular)**, or a crowbar, or a facón and thirty seconds of honest work. Inside, wrapped in oilcloth:

**LOS TRES TOMOS DEL DIARIO DE DON RUPERTO CORVALÁN.**

- Reading all three: **2 days** of study. In a one-shot, allow a player who spends thirty minutes in the house to skim tomo III (the useful one) and get the substance.
- **Cthulhu Mythos +4%** for the full read; **SAN 1D4 per volume.**
- **Contents**: forty years of a man documenting, in a dry commercial hand, the systematic project of not dying. Property, hides, tallow, silver — and interleaved with the accounts, the other work. The Hermandad. The chapel. The names of men who are all long dead. And, in tomo III, the ritual: **"Invocar al Abridor de los Caminos."** Learning it takes **2D6 weeks** and is not a thing that happens tonight; note it on the sheet and let it haunt a future campaign.

**HANDOUT 4 — Del tomo III del diario de Corvalán, 1806**

> *No es morir lo que me repugna. Es irse. He puesto cuarenta años en esta casa y no la he de dejar por una formalidad de la carne. Que me tapien abajo, con la tierra contra la espalda y la casa entera encima, y que la casa me sirva de cuerpo cuando el cuerpo no sirva. Los vecinos rezarán. Que recen. Yo he de estar debajo del piso que ellos pisan, y he de durar más que sus nietos, y aquel que aguarda en lo oscuro sabrá dónde encontrarme.*

**MECHANICAL TRIGGER — READ THIS**: the moment any investigator opens the alacena and touches the diaries, **Corvalán wakes properly.** Immediately, from directly overhead:

> *Arriba, en el cuarto del fondo, algo golpea. Tres veces, fuerte, contra el piso. La lámpara de la despensa tiembla y le cae polvo del techo encima.*

That is the starting gun for the upper floor. Everything above gets louder from here.

#### 6 — EL CUARTO DE TRASTOS
Broken furniture, a dismantled bedstead, crates. Excellent firewood. **Hidden in it**: nails, glass, a snapped scythe blade. Anyone rummaging without care rolls **Luck** — failure is **1D3 damage** and a nasty cut. Anyone taking a moment says so and takes nothing.

#### 7 — EL PRIMER Y SEGUNDO PATIO, Y EL ALJIBE
The second patio holds the **aljibe** — a brick rain-cistern with a low wellhead and a bucket on a rope, three *varas* deep.

**Establish first**: the cistern is fed by roof rain. It should be fresh. **It smells of salt water and low tide**, in a city where the river is fresh and forty blocks away. Nobody can explain this. **Natural World or Science (Regular)** confirms that this is not possible.

**LA ESCENA DEL ALJIBE** — run it the first time any investigator leans over the wellhead with a light:

> *El agua está quieta y negra y devuelve la luz del farol y devuelve tu cara. Y atrás de tu cara, a tres varas de hondo, en un aljibe de agua de lluvia donde no hay nada, dos luces rojas se abren despacio y te miran.*

Roll **Luck**:
- **Éxito** — a drop falls from the bucket rope and breaks the surface, and the image goes, and afterwards it is impossible to be sure. **SAN 1** for the uncertainty, which is worse than the certainty.
- **Fracaso** — the investigator wants, more than they have wanted anything today, **to touch the water.** They lean in. They go over the lip and into the cistern. **1D3 damage** on the way down, **SAN 1/1D6**, and now they are standing chest-deep in cold salt water in the dark with whatever they were looking at. Getting out is a **Climb (Regular)** or a rope and a friend. *There is nothing in the cistern.* There was never anything in the cistern. That is not a comfort and should not be delivered as one.

**Pulp note**: do not kill anyone in the aljibe. It's a scare and a soak, not a drowning.

### PLANTA ALTA

*Up the stairs off the first patio. The stairs are sound. Nothing about the stairs is a trap — save the bad footing for the sótano, where it counts.*

#### 8 — EL DORMITORIO PRINCIPAL
Double bed, wardrobe, washstand. Crucifixes again, thickest in this room. On the nightstand, a rosary and a *breviario*, both left behind — and a woman does not leave her rosary behind unless she leaves at speed.

**Under the bed (Spot Hidden)**: a chalk line drawn on the floorboards all the way around the bed frame, gone over many times, the last few passes shaky. Somebody in this room was drawing a circle around themselves every night and it was not working.

**SAN 0/1.**

#### 9 — EL CUARTO DE LOS CHICOS
Two small beds. A wooden horse. Lead soldiers, arranged. A rag doll.

**Spot Hidden**: the lead soldiers are not scattered — they are **in a line across the doorway**, facing out, a small ineffectual garrison set by somebody who was seven and had thought about this carefully. There is no supernatural effect. It is just the worst thing in the house.

**SAN 0/1.**

#### 10 — EL CUARTO DE HUÉSPEDES — **THE ATTACK ROOM**
*Corvalán's stage. This is the famous room and it should be the loudest ten minutes of the night.*

> *El cuarto del fondo. Una cama de hierro con el elástico a la vista y sin colchón. Una silla. Una ventana clavada. Y el olor — acá el olor no aparece y se va, acá el olor vive: pescado podrido, carne podrida, algo dulce por abajo que es peor que las dos cosas.*

This is where the second family's son hanged himself. It is where the knocking comes from. It is where Corvalán expends magic points to make a point.

**Corvalán's repertoire in this room** — use them in this order, escalating, one per round or one per stubborn player action. **He spends no more than 6 MP on the whole show**; this is theatre, not the fight.

| Manifestation | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Los golpes** | Three heavy blows on the floor, then the walls, then all four walls at once. Audible **throughout the house** — everyone downstairs hears it too. No SAN, but roll something and look grim. |
| **La sangre** | Blood wells through the ceiling plaster and runs down the walls in sheets. It is warm. **SAN 1** each time. It leaves no stain afterwards. |
| **La ventana** | Scratching on the outside of a window that is two floors up, nailed shut, and faces a blank wall. Then the frame begins to rattle. **SAN 1.** |
| **El olor** | Overwhelming — anyone who fails a **CON roll** retches and is at a penalty die for one round. |
| **LA CAMA** | **The set piece.** The iron bedstead lifts, turns, and **charges** the nearest investigator at the speed of a running horse. Corvalán rolls **POW×5** to hit; a hit is **1D6+2 damage**. If the target is between the bed and the window, they go **through the boarded window with it** — a further **1D6+2**, and they land in the rear garden two floors down among the wreckage of the parral. **SAN 1/1D4.** |

**Pulp handling of the bed**: let them **dodge it**. Let them duck and let it go through the wall. Let someone catch a friend's belt as they go past the window frame. If an investigator declares something absurd and heroic — riding the bed, wedging the chair under it, throwing a poncho over the whole thing — **let it work**, once, and make it the moment everyone remembers.

**Escaping the room**: the door swings shut and holds. It is not locked; something is holding it. **Opposed STR** against Corvalán's **STR 90**, or two investigators pushing together (bonus die), or an axe. He lets go the instant the theatre is over, and there is nothing more satisfying at the table than four people hitting a door that suddenly isn't held.

#### 11 — LA AZOTEA
Flat roof, low parapet, laundry lines, a view over Monserrat and the drums somewhere south. **The only place in the house where nothing happens.** Give it to them. A party that retreats to the azotea to argue and re-light lanterns should get five uninterrupted minutes, and should feel the difference when they go back down.

### EL SÓTANO

*Where it actually is. Gabriela told them; the diaries told them; the will told them. Everything above was to keep them from coming down here.*

**La puerta del sótano**, off the kitchen: barred **from the kitchen side** by the Macario family — Vitorio's sleepwalking is why. The bar is new pine and out of place in a house full of old wood.

> *La escalera baja doce escalones de madera que no vio el sol desde el año ocho. El tercero, el séptimo y el noveno están podridos hasta el alma y se van a dar cuenta cuando ya sea tarde.*

**Bajar**: **DEX roll or Climb (Regular)**. Failure is a fall — **1D6 damage**. A success may then help the next investigator down for a **bonus die**; a *pushed* attempt that fails hurts the helper too. Roll this once per person and then stop rolling it — it is a texture beat, not a gauntlet.

**Abajo**: packed earth floor, brick walls, ankle-deep cold, and the smell from upstairs at its source.

#### 12 — EL CUARTO DE HERRAMIENTAS — **EL FACÓN**
Tools, coils of rope, lengths of pipe, scrap timber, a keg of nails.

**Spot Hidden (Regular)**, or anyone who spends a minute rummaging (in which case no roll — but see below):

> *Entre los fierros hay un cuchillo que no es de esta casa. Es un facón viejo, de hoja ancha, con cabo de plata labrada y un trabajo que no se hace más. Está entero. No tiene una mota de herrumbre. Lo que tiene encima, negro y seco y en capas, no es herrumbre.*

**SAN 1/1D5** for what the layers are.

**EL FACÓN DE CORVALÁN** — the animate blade, and the scenario's best trick. **The moment it is seen, it is live.** Corvalán can act through it anywhere in the house for **1 magic point per round**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Fighting** | 50% (Hard 25 / Extreme 10) |
| **Dodge** | 17% (Hard 8 / Extreme 3) |
| **Damage** | 1D4+2 — **or 6+1D4+2** on an Extreme success, driving through armour and bone |
| **Cost to Corvalán** | 1 MP per round of action |
| **Defence** | It can be blocked. **A stiff cowhide, a wooden batea, a tin lid** — anything held up between you and it grants a **bonus die to Dodge**. Say this out loud when someone gets cut; the players will find something. |

**Killing it**: you can't, exactly. You can **pin it** (through the hilt into a beam — a called shot, Hard), **bury it**, **throw it in the aljibe**, or **wait Corvalán out of magic points**. A pulp table will do something better than any of these; let them.

#### 13 — LA CARBONERA
Empty charcoal store, black dust an inch thick over everything. A door in the outer wall to the street-level coal chute, **nailed shut from the inside** like everything else.

**Spot Hidden (Regular)**, or a **Hard** roll if they're rushing: the far wall's brick **does not match**. Different mortar, different bond, laid by someone in a hurry. It sounds hollow.

> *La pared del fondo de la carbonera no es la pared del fondo. El ladrillo es otro, la mezcla es otra, y el que la levantó la levantó rápido y no la levantó para que dure: la levantó para tapar una boca. Golpeen. Suena a hueco.*

Breaking through: any tool and two minutes. No roll. **Make them decide to do it**, and give them a beat of silence before they swing.

#### 14 — EL NICHO DE CORVALÁN
> *Detrás de la pared hay un cuarto que no está en ningún plano. Piso de tierra. Contra un costado, un escritorio de comerciante, con papeles encima que se deshacen si los mirás fuerte — cartas natales, tablas, cielos dibujados a mano para noches que ya pasaron hace cuarenta años.*
>
> *Y en el suelo, boca arriba, con las manos cruzadas y una cadena de hierro pesada enroscada al cuello, hay un hombre de dos varas de largo. No tiene un pelo en ninguna parte. La piel se le pegó al hueso y se le puso del color del sebo viejo. Los ojos son dos platos amarillos sin párpado. Los dientes le crecieron para afuera de la boca y le siguen creciendo.*
>
> *Huele a maíz podrido, dulce, y el olor los está esperando desde el año ocho.*

**LAS RATAS**: dozens, in the walls and the earth floor. They do not attack unless the party blocks the exit or attacks them first, and **killing one makes the rest flee.** They are an atmosphere and a nuisance, not an encounter.

**SAN 1/1D8** the moment Corvalán **moves**, which he does about four seconds after they get a good look at him.

**On a loss of 5+ SAN**: roll **INT** — success means the investigator understands exactly what they are looking at, and gets an involuntary reaction (1D4): 1 — **huye** blindly, shoving anyone in the way (1D2 damage to them); 2 — **paralizado** one full round; 3 — **suelta el arma**; 4 — **se desmaya** for 1D3 rounds. Then a normal bout of madness. **Cthulhu Mythos +5%.**

## DON RUPERTO CORVALÁN

*Play him as **spiteful, patient, and personal**. He is not a force of nature and he is not a mystery. He is a rich old man who arranged not to leave, and he has been alone under a floor since 1808, and he is delighted that somebody finally came down.*

**Key Hook**: he talks. He is the only Mythos-adjacent thing in this document that talks, and he uses it to **divide** — praising one investigator, mocking another, telling a third what the others said about them on the stairs. He wants them at each other's throats and he will settle for their attention. He covets Mythos knowledge brought into his house; if the party carried the *Liber Ivonis* in here, he wants it, and he will bargain for it.

| STR | CON | SIZ | INT | POW | DEX | APP | EDU | SAN |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | 115 | 55 | 80 | 90 | 35 | 05 | 80 | 0 |

**HP** 16 · **DB** +1D4 · **Build** 1 · **Move** 8 · **MP** 18 · **Armadura** 0 base, **11 from Escudo de Carne** (see below) · **Ataques** 1 por asalto

**Skills**: Cthulhu Mythos 17%, Intimidate 64%, Listen 60%, Sleight of Hand 30%, Stealth 72%

**Ataques**: garras/manos 55%, **1D6+DB**

**Hechizos**:

- **DOMINAR** — 1 MP, 1 round to cast. Opposed **POW**. On a success the target is dazed for **1D6+1 rounds** and obeys a single telepathic command. They will do violent, reckless, or self-endangering things; they **will not** kill themselves. They remember nothing afterwards. *Use this on the investigator carrying the lantern, or the one holding the rope.*
- **ESCUDO DE CARNE** — **already cast before the party ever entered the house.** Each MP spent grants **1D6 armour** against non-magical attacks; he has spent enough for **11 points**, which erode one-for-one as they absorb damage. This is why the first musket ball does nothing and the fourth one works, and the players should get to feel that curve.

**Combate**:
- **Fuego a quemarropa** gives the shooter a bonus die — he is slow (DEX 35) and does not dodge well.
- **Superioridad numérica**: 2+ attackers on him grant a bonus die.
- **La enfermedad de las uñas**: any investigator wounded by his claws rolls **Luck**. **Failure** — delirium onset in 1D10 days, **1D10 CON loss**, and the CON loss is **permanent**. **Success** — delirium for 1D6 days, no CON loss. Repeat weekly until recovery or death.
  - *Pulp option, recommended*: the source itself suggests waiving the long-term claw effects. In a one-shot, **do**. Make the roll at the table for the drama, narrate a bad week, and let everyone keep their CON.
- **Animarse**: getting up and moving costs him **2 MP for 5 combat rounds.**

**Cómo se lo mata, en la práctica**: burn him, break him, or bury him. Fire works. A shovel through the neck works. The pulp answer that every table finds sooner or later — **collapse the nicho on top of him and salt the ground** — works, and is the correct ending. Reducing him to 0 HP ends him permanently **provided the body is destroyed or the nicho sealed**; leave him intact on the floor and Braña has a problem again in six months.

## EL DESENLACE

*Three outcomes, per the original. Don't editorialise about which was correct.*

### ÉXITO — lo terminaron
Corvalán destroyed, the nicho opened and burned or resealed. Braña pays on the spot, in silver, out of the strongbox, and is **enormously, embarrassingly grateful** — a soft man who has just understood that he sent people into something.

> *—Yo no creía. Que quede dicho también: yo no creía, y les pagué igual, y ahora les voy a pagar el doble y no me lo discutan. Ustedes hagan lo que quieran con la plata. Yo esa casa la voy a tirar abajo y voy a vender el terreno pelado.*

He does exactly that. There is a lot in Monserrat with nothing on it for years afterward and nobody builds on it.

### FRACASO PARCIAL — informaron que no había nada
The investigators sign the statement, take the twenty pesos, and go. **Braña sleeps in the house** to prove the point to a buyer.

They find him in the sótano four days later, at the foot of the rotten stairs, with **thirty-one wounds** from an old wide-bladed knife that is nowhere in the building.

And then it is the investigators' problem, because they were the last people in the house, they were paid by the deceased, they broke his front door in front of the whole street, and they are **armed men from the campaña in a city that already believes the worst of armed men from the campaña.** The Jefatura is new, thin, and eager to solve something. Proving innocence is the epilogue, and it is a hard one, and it should be played out at least in outline — five minutes of a nervous young officer asking why their statement says the house was sound.

### FRACASO TOTAL — no salieron
The house takes them the way it took the others. Braña sells it in 1824 to a family from Córdoba.

## RECOMPENSAS

### 1 — Cordura
- **+1D10 SAN** if Corvalán was destroyed or permanently sealed.
- **+1D6 SAN** if the party survived and left the house understanding what was in it, even without finishing it.
- **Ninguna** on the partial-failure ending — signing a paper you know is false is not a resolution and the sheet should say so.
- **+1D4 additional** if the Macario family's situation is materially improved: Gabriela believed and discharged, Vitorio moved somewhere better, the children written to. Any investigator who *bothers* gets this.

### 2 — Chequeos de Experiencia
Standard 7e. Tick every skill successfully used; roll each against its current value at the end; a **failure** grants **+1D10%**. Likely tonight: Library Use, Persuade, Fast Talk, Spot Hidden, Psychology, Climb, Locksmith, History, Occult, Fighting, Firearms, Dodge, First Aid. Keep a scratch list during play.

### 3 — Objetos con nombre

**El facón de Corvalán** — *Unlock*: Corvalán is destroyed and somebody had the nerve to keep the knife.

A wide-bladed facón with a worked-silver hilt, older than anyone can date, which has never rusted and never will. Once its master is gone it is **an inert, superb weapon**: **1D6+1+DB**, and it **never breaks and never needs sharpening.** But it is not *quite* inert. Once per session, at a moment of the Keeper's choosing and always at the worst one, its bearer finds it **already in their hand** when they were certain it was sheathed. No mechanical effect. Just that.

**El Liber Ivonis** — *Unlock*: recovered from the chapel crypt and carried out.

A third-legible Latin hand-copy with Spanish marginalia. **+2% Cthulhu Mythos** on a full study; contains "Invocar al Abridor de los Caminos" (**2D6 semanas** to learn) if the diaries were also taken, since the diaries hold the half the manuscript is missing. A genuinely dangerous object to hand a player who wants it. **Give it to them.** That is what it's for.

**La carta de Gabriela Macario** — *Unlock*: she is believed, and treated as a witness rather than a patient, and somebody goes back to tell her it's over.

Not an item — a **contact**. A seamstress with a very clear head, out of the Convalecencia and working again, who now knows what the world actually contains and would like to be useful. She writes. She keeps writing. Worth more than the facón in any campaign that runs past tonight.
